# R-174: A fleet ordered to a far coast lands as it would next door

- **Decided:** 2026-11-15, a maintainer
- **Status:** Confirmed
- **Source:** Question 66: a fleet lands on the coast next door, but not on a far coast it is ordered to: which is right? Answer: option 1, the road lands her where the step next door would. Question 170 (2026-11-28): does a fleet alone that chases a quarry ashore land, as a Move does? Answer: option 1, the chase stops at the shore, as built; the maintainers' answer of 28 November.
- **Enforced by:** [`a-fleet-ordered-to-a-far-coast-lands-as-it-would-next-door`](../../tests/scenario/a-fleet-ordered-to-a-far-coast-lands-as-it-would-next-door.test.ts)

One rule for the step, however far the target: a column of ships alone that may land on the coast next door may
also land when its target is a far coast. Example: Admiral Wren leads only a Galleon Fleet at sea. Ordered to a coast
two seas away, she sails there and lands, as she lands when ordered onto the coast next door. The order form's forecast
follows.

A chase isn't a Move: a column of ships alone that chases a quarry ashore stops at the shore. A hero who wants to
follow sends a Move. Admiral Hale's Galleons chase a raider who lands on the Vakfield coast; the chase ends at sea.

The question was: the step next door and the road to a far target disagree. Option 1: "the road lets her land where
the step next door would".
