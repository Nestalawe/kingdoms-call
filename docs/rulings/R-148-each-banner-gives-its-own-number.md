# R-148: Each banner gives the strength its own text says

- **Decided:** 2026-10-31, a maintainer
- **Status:** Decided, not built
- **Source:** Question 20: the banners' texts give more strength than the battle does: which is right? Answer: option 1, each banner gives its own number. Question 177 (asked in #855): does an older Standard of the Nine Legions, whose words say +5, give +5 or +6? Answer: +6, re-stamped with today's words; the maintainers' answer of 28 November. The older Battle Standard too (its words say +2): +4, re-stamped; a maintainer's answer in the session, 28 November.
- **Enforced by:** [`each-banner-gives-the-strength-its-own-text-says-and-of-two-banners-the-stronger-counts`](../../tests/unit/each-banner-gives-the-strength-its-own-text-says-and-of-two-banners-the-stronger-counts.test.ts)

Each banner gives the armies its bearer leads the strength its own text says: the two relic standards (the
Battle Standard and the Standard of the Nine Legions) give more than the ordinary banners. Two banners don't add up:
the stronger counts. Example: Lady Wren's king carries the Standard of the Nine Legions, and every army he leads fights
+6, its text's number (the Battle Standard gives +4, an ordinary banner +2). A king who
carries both relic standards gets +6, not +10.

Every copy of the two relic standards already in a running game takes its row's strength and words at the start of
each turn: an older Standard of the Nine Legions, whose words say +5, gives +6 and says so; an older Battle Standard,
whose words say +2, gives +4.

**Note:** Decided, not yet built: the re-stamp of the older relic standards. Today an older copy gives the number its
own words say.

The question was: the relics' texts give more than the battle did. Option 1: "each banner gives its own number" ("the
relics were made to be stronger, and the text is what players read").
