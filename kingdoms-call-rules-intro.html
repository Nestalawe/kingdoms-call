Part One · The Basics

What the Game Is

Kingdoms Call is a fantasy strategy game played by email. You rule a realm on a shared, hidden map. Each turn you write orders for your king and heroes; once everyone has sent theirs, the world resolves them all at once and sends you a private Chronicle of what your people saw and did.

You grow by exploring the fog, conquering the neutral provinces that fill it, clearing the monster lairs hidden in the wild, recruiting armies and training heroes — while rival realms do the same.

There is no turn limit. From turn 10 onward, a king whose realm dominates the world may Call the Reckoning. Two turns later the world is judged on thirteen Imperial Titles, and the realm with the greatest legacy is crowned. More below.

How a Turn Works

A turn is a season made of five phases. Each of your characters gets one order per phase — up to five orders a turn — and carries them out in order, phase 1 through 5. Everyone in the world acts in phase 1 before anyone acts in phase 2, so when you do something matters as much as what you do.

  • A blank phase is a Defend order. A character you give nothing to keeps watch where they stand.
  • Within a phase, non-moving orders (casting, recruiting, practising, searching) resolve first, then Moves and the battles they cause. Faster characters — higher initiative, the sum of all their skill levels — act before slower ones.
  • At the end of the turn tax is collected and wages paid in one step, diplomacy takes effect, and your Chronicle is written.
  • Missed the deadline? Your characters simply Defend for the turn.
The one timing rule to rememberA province you conquer in phase 3 pays you tax at the end of that same turn. Conquest can pay for itself within the turn.

Setting Up Your Realm

Before the game begins you make four choices:

  • Race — Human, Elven, Dwarven or Orcish. Sets the troops you raise at home and a couple of racial gifts. Summary below.
  • Alignment — Divine, Good, Druidic, Neutral, Pagan, Evil or Undead. Sets your battle zeal, hiring costs and a unique signature action. Summary below.
  • Your king's temper — Berserk, Brave, Cautious or Cowardly: how they fight in personal combat. Cowardly is the safest choice for a king, and losing your king hurts.
  • Your king's skills — 4 points to spread across the fifteen skills. A point of Sage starts realm-wide discoveries early; Tactical makes your armies hit harder; a magic school gives you spells from turn one.

What you start with

  • A home province — your richest, fully explored, paying 10 gold a turn and able to recruit every land unit of your race.
  • A treasury of 20 gold.
  • Your king with a small starting army, and two heroes with 3 random skill points each.

Part Two · The World

The Map

The world is a hex grid of provinces, hidden under fog of war until your characters visit or scout them. You always see the provinces you own, the ones your characters stand in, and the basics of those next door.

  • Terrain — plains, forest, jungle, mountain, desert or sea. Cavalry fights badly in forest and mountains; mountains squeeze the battle line so big armies can't all fight; only fleets (or troops carried by fleets, or flyers) can enter sea.
  • Tax — every province pays gold each turn you hold it: poor 2–3, prosperous 4–6, rich 7–10. Richer provinces have bigger neutral guards (roughly 1–2, 2–3 and 3–4 defenders).
  • Neutral guards — most unclaimed provinces are held by a small garrison. To take one you must beat it in battle with an army; a lone hero can only walk into an empty province. Taking a defended province often takes two phases.
  • Exploration — each province has an exploration percentage for you. Exploring reveals its wealth, defenders and hidden lairs.
  • Lairs — monster dens, ruins and treasure sites, on land and at sea. Clearing them is the main source of gold early, of magic items, and the only way heroes gain maximum hit points. See Lairs & Duels.

Part Three · Your People

Your King & Heroes

Your characters — the king and your heroes — are the only pieces you give orders to. Everything said of heroes applies to the king too. More heroes means more orders each turn, which is one of the biggest advantages in the game.

  • Hit points are earned, not trained. Everyone starts at 15 HP (18 for Orcs). Maximum HP rises only by winning fights — lair guardians and duels. Wounds heal by the Rest order, not on their own.
  • If your king dies, an heir is crowned next turn in your wealthiest province — but a green one. Your realm ends only if your king dies with no land left. Be careful with the king.
  • Heroes cost wages: total skill levels ÷ 4, rounded up (a starting hero pays 1 gold a turn). Kings draw no wage.
  • Wandering heroes roam the map. Send a character to their province and Hire Hero for 2–4 gold a turn — cheaper the closer their alignment is to yours.

Skills in Brief

There are fifteen skills, each running from 0 to 9. Levels 1–5 can be trained with the Practice Skill order or earned by doing the thing; levels 6–9 come only from experience and unlock special perks. A skill at 0 does nothing at all — one level in a new skill is often worth more than another in one you have.

SkillWhat it does for you
Martial
MeleeHit harder and more often in personal combat (+10% to hit per level).
ArcheryShoot better in personal combat; sharpens your army's pre-battle volley (+5% missile strength per level).
Tactical+20% army strength per level (×2.0 at level 5) in land battles. The best free multiplier in the game.

Naval+20% per level to fleets you lead, and more sea movement.

SpySpy orders succeed more (20% + 20% per level); you are harder to spot in enemy land.
ThiefLets you Steal gold and items (needs Thief 1).
DruidLets you lead troops of other races (Druid 1) and wild creatures and monsters (Druid 2–5).
SageLets you Research permanent realm-wide discoveries (5% chance per level per order; Neutral realms double).
ExplorerChart provinces much faster and find their lairs sooner.
Magic — five schools
White · Psychic · Illusory · Elemental · NecromancyLevel 1 in a school gives you all five of its spells. Casting succeeds at 30% + 10% per level. See Magic in Brief.

Practising a skill succeeds 25% of the time at level 0→1, falling to 5% at 4→5; two of your characters practising the same skill in the same province help each other, and a more-skilled mentor present makes the gain automatic.

Part Four · Giving Orders

The Orders You'll Use

There are many orders; these dozen are the ones your first turns are built from.

OrderWhat it does
MoveStep into one adjacent province. Marching an army into a defended province starts a battle; win, and you take it. One Move always = one province; your movement points decide how many Moves you can make across the turn.
DefendKeep watch for the rest of the turn over your province and every neighbouring province you own. If an enemy army marches into one of them, your general rides there first and fights as the defender. A blank phase is a Defend.
Recruit ArmiesRaise units in a province you hold — normally one of each type per province per turn (two at your home; two garrison units or two fleets anywhere).
ExploreChart the province you stand in, revealing its lairs and defenders.
Search LairFight the guardian of a discovered lair in your province for gold, an item and toughness. Check the lair's grade first.
Hunting RumoursFor 1–4 gold per lair, learn a guardian's weakness, its hoard, and your own odds of beating it. Cheap insurance before a Search.
Practice SkillTrain a skill toward level 5.
Cast SpellWork one of your school's spells on a character or province.
Research(Needs Sage 1.) Roll for a permanent realm-wide discovery.
RestRecover hit points (30% of maximum).
Leave / Transfer Army & Pick Up ArmyDrop units as a province garrison or hand them to another of your characters there; take command of unled garrison units where you stand.
Hire HeroTake a wandering hero in your province into service.
SpyLearn what is really in the province you stand in — defenders, owner, lairs, characters.

Each alignment also has one signature action of its own — see Races & Alignments. Orders like Encounter Character, Steal, Send Item and Equip Items exist too; you'll find them in the Comprehensive Rulebook when you need them.

Part Five · War

Armies & Gold

Armies are made of units. A character leads a stack of them; units can also be left in a province as its garrison. Which units a province can raise depends on its race and terrain — your home province offers every land unit of your race.

Quality is the biggest lever

A unit's real strength is its base strength × quality: Green ×1, Average ×2 (new recruits), Veteran ×3, Crack ×4, Elite ×5. Units are promoted by winning battles and by their commander practising Tactical (or Naval for fleets), and demoted by losing.

The kinds of unit

  • Foot — the battle line; steady on almost any ground.
  • Cavalry — strong in open country, poor in forest, jungle and mountain.
  • Archers — fire a volley before the lines close.
  • Flyers — cross any terrain and even sea without ships.
  • Garrison — 1 gold, cheap static defence; always goes straight into the province's garrison.
  • Fleets — the only units that fight at sea, and the only way to carry armies across it (one unit per ship, two for light foot).
  • Creatures & Monsters — powerful wild units that need the Druid skill to lead.
The wage rule — read this twiceEvery unit costs its price to recruit, and the same price again every turn as its wage — garrisons included. Tax and wages settle in the same end-of-turn step, so a unit costs double in its first season. If you can't pay, your greenest units disband on their own, then heroes desert. Keep your wage bill comfortably under your income.

Leading other races needs Druid 1 — recruits take the race of the province they were raised in, so a conquered foreign province gives you foreign troops. Troops of your own race fight +20% under your crown and another +20% under a leader of their own race.

Battles in Brief

You never roll dice; the engine fights the battle and your Chronicle shows the working. Each side's total strength is compared, luck deals one swing, and the stronger side usually wins.

  • How sure is a win? Twice the enemy's strength wins about 80% of the time; three times, about 90%; four times, always. A slight edge is nearly a coin-flip.
  • Defenders fight at ×1.25 on their own ground (×1.5 at a home capital).
  • Tactical multiplies your whole land army: ×1.2 per level, up to ×2.0.
  • Terrain sets the width of the front line — 6 units on plains, desert or sea, 4 in forest or jungle, 3 in mountains. Units behind the front fight at half strength or less, so huge stacks are wasted on tight ground.
  • Losing costs units and quality; the more decisive the defeat, the worse. A beaten force retreats to a friendly province next door — with nowhere to run, it suffers more.
  • Battles can carry on into the next phase if the loser doesn't break — budget two phases to take a contested province.

Lairs & Duels

When two characters fight — in a lair, an ambush or a duel before a battle — it's personal combat: rounds of arrows and blows shaped by Melee, Archery, hit points, items, spells and temper. A character beaten badly enough can die.

Lair grades — send the right hero

Lairs come in four grades, and the guardian's grade tells you who to send. A hero of the right measure wins about 19 fights in 20; a hero one grade short can simply be killed.

GradeHero you need (roughly)Reward
Weak15 HP, about 5 skill points (e.g. Melee 3, Archery 2)5–8 gold, a Weak item
Moderate~30 HP, Melee 6, a few Weak items12–18 gold, a Moderate item
Powerful~45 HP, Melee 7, Moderate items20–30 gold, a Powerful item
Legendary~60 HP, Melee 9, Powerful items40–60 gold, a Legendary relic

Every guardian has a weakness — a school of magic, archery, a race or an alignment — that doubles the damage against it. Hunting Rumours tells you what it is, and your own odds, before you commit. No guardian's single blow can take more than a third of your maximum HP.

Temper in one line each

  • Berserk — hits hardest, dies fastest.
  • Brave — the complete fighter; gets a second wind once per fight.
  • Cautious — hardest to kill; punishes attackers who miss. Breaks off a lair fight below 40% health.
  • Cowardly — poor swordsman, best archer and thief; may beg for mercy when felled. The safest king.

Part Six · Power

Magic in Brief

Five schools, each its own skill. Level 1 in a school gives you all five of its spells at once. Every cast is a roll — 30% + 10% per level, capped at 95% — and you may cast 1 action spell per school per turn at levels 1–2, 2 at level 3, 3 at levels 4–5.

✨ White

Healing and holiness — Resurrect a fallen hero, Bless a province for extra tax, Holy Symbol against the undead.

🧠 Psychic

The mind — Charm a hero, Oratory to lift an army's quality, Clairvoyance, Psychic Blast.

🎭 Illusory

Deception — Invisibility, Phantasmal Armies, Mirror Image, false terrain, hidden lairs.

🔥 Elemental

Destruction — Lightning, Fireball, Earthquake, Flood, and summoning an elemental.

💀 Necromancy

Death — raise Skeletal Armies from battlefield graves, Curse, Plague, Control Undead.

Spells are used three ways: as an action in a phase, in a personal fight (set in your encounter plan), or as battle magic to swing an army battle. Magic items from lairs boost skills, health, armies or your economy; a hero must equip worn items for them to count. Sage discoveries (from the Research order) are permanent realm-wide upgrades — the earlier you start, the more they pay.

The seven alignments

Alignment runs from holiest to unholiest — Divine, Good, Druidic, Neutral, Pagan, Evil, Undead. Before each battle your army rolls for zeal and fights at ×1.3 if it fires. Heroes of a distant alignment cost more to hire; Divine and Undead will never serve each other.

Part Eight · The Endgame

Diplomacy & Winning

Diplomacy

Realms are Neutral, Allied or at War with each other. Both sides must propose a treaty to move a step toward alliance; one declaration of war is enough to break it. Declarations take effect at the end of the turn — except from Evil and Undead realms, whose wars begin at once. Allies can march through each other's land and can jointly reach the dominance needed for the Reckoning, but they do not share the victory.

The Reckoning

From turn 10, a living king whose realm (with its direct allies) holds enough of the world's total tax base may Call the Reckoning. The bar is set by the number of realms — 45% in a four-realm game, 40% with six or more — and rises for each ally you bring (an ally never makes it cheaper). If nobody has called it by turn 15, the bar falls 5 points a turn to a floor of 25%. Your portal always shows your current bar.

The call gives rivals two full turns to break the claim by driving you back below the bar. If it holds, the world is judged on Legacy: thirteen Imperial Titles — the Throne (largest tax base, 20 points) and twelve others worth 10 each, for the best general, admiral, monster-slayer, scholar, mage, spy, explorer and so on — plus 1 point per Legendary relic held. The most Legacy wins. Standings are shown from turn 5.

What that means for youWinning is not just about being biggest. Every skill you push past 5, every discovery, every lair cleared and every battle won is building toward a Title. Keep a hand in several of them.

Part Nine · Strategy

Your First Three Turns

Two facts shape a good opening: every unit costs double in its first season, and a province taken this turn pays you this turn. So: recruit as much as your conquests will pay for, and no more.

Don't march out on turn 1 with your starting armyIt isn't enough to take even a lightly guarded province reliably. Recruit first.

Turn 1

  • Recruit at home — enough to make your first assault a near-sure thing, but not so much the wage bill sinks you.
  • Send a hero to the lair in your home province. Gold, toughness and maybe an item. Do this every turn there's a lair to clear.
  • Explore the province you mean to take next, so you can see its defenders.
  • Practise something with a spare order — Sage, Druid or Tactical.
  • Attack in phase 3 or 4, so a two-phase fight finishes inside the turn.

Turn 2

  • Take the poor province first — fewest defenders. Save richer ones for when your stack is bigger.
  • One strong stack beats two weak ones. Don't split.
  • You now hold two provinces — recruit in both, wages still under income.

Turn 3

  • Garrison what you take — an empty province is retaken by a single enemy hero.
  • Train Tactical on your main general — once your stacks are five or six units it's the best multiplier you can buy without gold.
  • Pick a direction — the wild buys time; a neighbour invites war.
  • Watch the wage line every turn.

Ten things new players get wrong

  1. Marching out on turn 1 with the starting army.
  2. Spending all their gold on turn 1 — the wage bill lands the same turn.
  3. Forgetting garrisons cost wages too.
  4. Splitting forces.
  5. Attacking mountains with a huge stack — only three units fight in front.
  6. Marching cavalry into forest.
  7. Expecting to take a province in one phase — budget two.
  8. Leaving a conquered province empty.
  9. Ignoring lairs — the cheapest gold and the only source of hero HP and items.
  10. Sending a hero into a lair a grade above their measure.
The habit that winsEvery turn: clear a lair, take a province you can take safely, keep wages under income, and spend one spare order on a skill.
