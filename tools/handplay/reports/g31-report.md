## Each alignment, and how round 26 played

**Evil (R0 won; R7 wiped out).**
- R0 shows what a monster province does under the new rules. It made a Dark Pact every second turn (turns 5, 7, 9, 13, 15 and 19), and the cooldown held exactly. Each pact cost 10.5g and 6 to 10 HP.
- R0's Titan and Basilisk provinces bred into their own garrisons. Half wages now apply only to those home garrisons, so the bred beasts in the king's host paid full. R0 still ended with 347g.
- The one-company altar promotion fired four times (Gnaflats, Lithwaste, Highstead and Khazridge), lifting one company each time to Crack, Veteran, Crack and Elite. It is now a bonus, not an engine.
- R7 had no monster province. It made the Dark Pact from afar four times, at 12g and 9 HP each. Good R2's king held Ironvines against it under the Shield, and R2 then took R7's capital and every province after it. R7 ended at 0g, disbanding trolls it could not pay.

**Divine (wiped out).**
- Divine Power rose 0 to 1 to 2 to 3: one point per three consecrations, plus the shining holy site Gnaflats. A report line came with every rise and fall, and the cap of 6 was never close.
- Sanctuary, Grace abounding and Revelation were each listed, but none was seen to do anything. No Divine hero ended a turn wounded on holy ground, so Sanctuary's healing never had cause to fire.
- The Proclaimed Crusade worked: "the sword consecrates" made Lithwaste holy the moment it was retaken. The Crusade's ×1.5 still lost every battle to Evil's Titan host. Five consecrated provinces fell in six turns, one altar promotion each.

**Undead (lost its capital).**
- Cross-training worked. Both heroes reached Necromancy 4 on turn 2 and raised two or three skeletons a cast at the capital barrow. The king, at Necromancy 1, fizzled most of his casts.
- **The round-26 garrison rule held:**
  - From turn 4 the capital barrow kept 4 to 13 unled skeletons.
  - The Khazheight barrow kept 10 from turn 12, and by turn 19 it held 28 that Morwenna then took up.
  - Blighted Magscar raised and kept its own.
- A small skeleton garrison does not hold land against a host. Blighted Urwells, with three companies, fell to Evil's 10 on turn 17. Its surviving skeleton fell back to ordinary Zogtangle and crumbled there, with a clear line.
- Bind the Bones to Crack bit early: everything was Crack by turn 9, and later binds cost 10 to 14g. At 1g a skeleton money was never short: the Undead ended with 248g and nothing to spend it on. The Undead's mistake was attacking a Druid capital that had just been grown into forest: 22 companies went in and 11 were lost, with the Druids losing none.

**Pagan (fourth, 11%).**

## Report and order problems (new)

1. **A pact notice cannot be answered in time.** The owner learns of it only from the next report, and the seal at phase 0 of that turn resolves before any move the owner makes.
2. **The refusal checks run hero first, war second.** A realm that is both at war and has a hero in the province only ever sees the hero reason.
3. **A skeleton garrison that withdraws from a lost blighted or barrow province into an ordinary one crumbles at once** (Urwells to Zogtangle). The line is clear. Whether the dead of the soil should be able to fall back at all is a design question.
4. **A challenge from a League hero who carries the ransom plan, against a king, is a duel to the death.** Nothing warns the player that kings cannot be ransomed. Two Neutral heroes and the Neutral king died this way.
5. **The Undead and Good realms ended with 248 to 531g** and nothing that changes a battle to buy. Bind is capped at Crack, and the pressed levy is small.
6. **A landless Divine king and two heroes stood idle for five turns.** Nothing in the kit lets the faith come back: no Grace for the realm, and no way to consecrate without land.

## Three balance observations

1. **An Evil realm with a monster province is still the strongest single army.** R0's Titan host, fed by a pact every other turn, never lost a battle and took two whole realms. Suggestion: a pact company arrives Green rather than Average, and a host may hold no more pact monsters than the king's Druid level. That would cap a king at Druid 3 to three pacts instead of six.
2. **Divine has no answer to a surprise war from Evil.** Holy defence and the Crusade's ×1.5 lost every battle to the Titan host, and five consecrated provinces fell in six turns. Suggestion: when an Evil or Undead host stands beside a consecrated province, that province raises one free Veteran levy of the realm's own race, a faith's version of the Beacons. Also, let a landless Divine king consecrate the province he stands in, if he takes it back.
3. **The pact notice needs to be answerable.** Suggestion: allow the pact to be sealed no sooner than two turns after the notice, or resolve the owner's hero moves into the province before the pact in that phase. Keep the double price.
