## 2. Each alignment

**Good (R0, R7).** What mattered: steady money and a Tactical 8 king. R7 took seven Evil provinces in four turns (two lost back) by walking into empty land while the Evil host sat in its capital. What did not: three assaults on that capital all failed. **Villages +2/+1:** both Good realms were never short (R0 42g→98g, R7 66g→124g over the last four turns) but neither was the richest: Undead 216g and Divine 163g finished above them. Good is comfortable, not dominant.

**Undead.** What mattered: Necromancy 3 on turn 1, 17 led companies by turn 5, the League capital on turn 9. Bind the Bones cost 34–48g an order at its peak. What did not: numbers in the mountains. King (25) and hero (21) lost 22 companies in two assaults on woken Stonestone on turn 18. Gold piled up unspent (190g–242g from turn 16).

**Divine.** What mattered: Power 6 from turn 10, and two Crusades (turns 10 and 17). On the second, three provinces were consecrated the moment they fell, and Evil R4 was finished. Ended with 12 consecrated provinces and three holy sites. What did not: the Crusade's hero hunt (the one chase was turned away by sentries).

**Pagan (R3, R9).** What mattered: Old Stones ×1.6 on a mountain saved R9 (10 companies beat 46), and Blood Harvest paid it 44g that turn (2g→48g). What did not: the storm as a siege tool. R3 stormed the Druid capital four times and never took it. **Round 28:** the caps always bound (2 destroyed, up to 4 demoted); the victims' lines name each company and read well; the second cast on Silverpass on turn 19 was refused with a clear line, and the cast was allowed again on turn 20. The rule works.

**Evil (R4, R8).** What mattered: eight Dark Pacts (five by R4, three by R8); every line showed "asks no wages for 3 turns". R8 took R4's capital on turn 11. What did not: the pact companies could not save R4, which then fought Divine and Evil at once and had no land by turn 19. **Solvency:** R4 failed wages on turns 13, 14 and 17, but only after it lost its capital (wages 62–73g on 48g). R8 stayed solvent to the end (9g), helped by the Host-Leader's −19g and 7g Tribute of Fear. The wage-free turns helped early; they do not carry a realm that is losing land. **HP floor:** on turn 13 R4's pact took 4 HP, not 7, and stopped at 7 of 18; no refusal line ever appeared. R8's last pact was refused for a different reason ("can lead no more beasts").

**Neutral (League).** What mattered: Trade Pact and patience. It fell to 2 provinces on turn 9 and ended with 4, capital regained. **Pact notice:** 3g each; the Undead were told the price (16g, then 40g) and how to block. They blocked Ironverge by marching a hero in before the third order, and failed to block Ashflats (I sent the hero to the wrong province). "One embassy at a time" showed on turn 15. Fair to both sides. **Ransom: never fired.** The League won one battle against a hero (turn 19, 12 v 11 at its old capital), but he lost 1 company of 11 and retreated, so no captive. So the hold, the 3g price and the fall-back are untested here.

**Druidic.** What mattered: a mountain capital and a Tactical 6 hero on Defend. They held five assaults at the capital (four Pagan, one Good with 16 companies) and beat two more columns by riding out on turn 20. Fell from 12 provinces to 6 through my wage error and a two-front war.

## 3. Report and order problems (new)

1. **A bought province reads as conquered.** After the pact, the seller sees both "The League has bought Ashflats from under you" and "Ashflats has been captured from you by King5 (Realm5), leading 9 units". No host entered; the king stood a province away.
2. **"its 0 companies are turned loose as a mercenary band standing in the province"** when the bought province had no garrison. Drop the clause when there is none.
3. **Crusade promise against outcome.** The proclamation says its heroes "run Realm4's heroes down without fail"; the same turn, "the sentries of their column are awake — the challenger is turned away".
4. **The loser sees the winner's income.** The Undead report carried the Pagan line "Blood Harvest — the old gods claim their due from 11 fallen enemy units: +22g in offerings", twice.
5. **Dark Pact's floor is silent.** "(6g, −4 HP → 7)" where earlier pacts cost 7 HP: nothing says the floor cut the price, or that the next pact would be refused.
6. **No bare-capital warning.** The Undead capital had no garrison and no hero from turn 17 to 20; no line said so. If it needs an enemy next door, the rule should say so.
7. **No word on why Ransom did not fire.** The League's victory report says nothing about the beaten hero escaping. One line ("he kept his column and got away") would teach the rule.
8. **A realm with no land plays on.** Evil R4 at 0 provinces still gives orders, sends peace overtures and gets "cannot pay 5g wages". No line says the realm has fallen.
9. **Treasury line order.** "Realm9 has 46g in the treasury" comes before taxes and after 44g of offerings; from 2g at the start it reads as an error until you find the offerings far above.

## 4. Balance observations

1. **Mountains beat numbers, so wars stall.** Fronts of 2 or 3 plus the defender's ×1.5 meant 46 Undead companies lost to 10, 16 Good to 8, and 12 Divine to 2 Cave Trolls and a small garrison (turn 15). The Druid capital outlasted two realms. *Suggestion:* let each failed assault on a province widen the next attacker's front there by 1 for two turns (a breach), or let a storm on a province widen the front by 1 that turn. That would also give the Pagan storm a siege use.
2. **Ransom's trigger is too narrow to meet.** In 20 turns the League won one battle against a hero, with no captive. A small realm rarely wins a battle in which the loser also loses half. *Suggestion:* also take the hero when the League wins and the beaten host is smaller than the League's by half or more at the start, or when the League wins as defender. Then test again.
3. **Winners end with gold they cannot use; losers die of wages.** Undead 216g, Divine 163g, Good 124g and Pagan R3 108g at the end, while three realms lost armies to wages. Villages are not the cause: Good was third and fourth in gold. *Suggestion:* leave Villages at +2/+1. For the piles, wait for the gold sinks Toby plans. For the collapses, a wage warning in the orders view ("next turn's wages 88g, income about 69g") would have stopped all three of my overspends.
