# Game g6: Undead, Evil, Divine, Good (seed 606, round 23 kits, 16 turns hand-played)

## Result
Nobody won in 16 turns. The Undead and the Divine ended level on land, the Divine far ahead on gold; Good was wiped off the map by turn 12; Evil lost and retook its capital in the last two turns.

| Realm | Turn 8: provinces / gold / companies | Turn 16: provinces / gold / companies | Tax vs wages at the end |
|---|---|---|---|
| Undead (Orcish) | 7 / 61g / 8 + 7 garrison | 12 / 145g / 10 + 9 garrison | 71 vs 67 |
| Evil (Dwarven) | 7 / 49g / 11 | 6 / 53g / 13 | 41 vs 36 |
| Divine (Human) | 6 / 29g / 10 + 4 garrison | 11 / 291g / 9 + 6 garrison | 91 (28 tithes) vs 41 |
| Good (Elven) | 4 / 9g / 4 + 6 garrison | 0 / 6g / king alone, 26 HP | - |

How it went: everyone took 3-4 neutral provinces by turn 5 (the attacks that failed, failed on 'luck' rolls at 67-88% edges). No war can be declared before turn 6, and only on a realm you have met, so the probes started turn 6. The Undead (Harvest the Field after every fight, Bind the Bones every turn, four blights) built a 12-company Crack/Elite host and broke it twice on Good's Shield (turns 7 and 11: 17 companies lost, none killed), then ground Good down province by province where the king was not, killed both Good heroes with a thief who became a Melee-4 duellist, and took the empty capital on the third try. Evil walked into consecrated Gromwells (turn 8) for three altar promotions, was repelled from the Divine capital, and then lost every battle to the Divine king's 13-company Crusade stack until Anvildown (turn 16), where the Divine lost 7 of 13 attacking. The Undead king lost a duel to the Good king at turn 14 and rose by Lichdom with no army: his 7 Elite skeletal companies dissolved.

## Per alignment

### Undead
- Used: Harvest the Field (every turn, 25+ companies raised: the engine of the realm), Bind the Bones (every turn; 2g a company; Average to Elite in four turns), Blight the Land (5 provinces: 24g a turn Grave Goods at the end, plus a free skeletal garrison company a turn each), Barrows (Magspire, Gashmoor: 3 free graves a turn each), The dead fear nothing (×1.6 with 2 blights, ×1.75 later), Lichdom (used once, at turn 14), two starting skeletal companies.
- Mattered: Harvest + Bind + Grave Goods. Grave Goods paid a third of the Undead's tax by turn 12 (24g of 71g) and the Host-Leader title paid most of the wages: money was never a problem after turn 4. Lichdom saved the king but not the host.
- Never found a use for: Desecrate and The soul is taken (never reached the Divine: a sea belt and Evil lay between), The dead of this soil's garrisons never fought.
- Strong: the economy. Weak: the host dies to any Shielded Good province, and the whole skeletal host dies with the king. Fun: very; the loop feels like necromancy.

### Evil
- Used: Terror (every victory; −20/−30% on neutrals took 7 provinces cheaply), Spread Dread (Wodan, Vaunt), Tribute of Fear (2-3g a turn), the altar promotions when casting down Gromwells (Crack, Elite, Veteran), a Mammoth creature province (Brokensands) for beast companies.
- Mattered: Terror on neutrals (the easy early land) and the altar promotions (its two best companies came from one empty consecrated province).
- Never found a use for: Dark Pact and Savage Fury (no monster province anywhere near; the two monster lairs it saw were Powerful or Legendary), The pits breed, Beast-masters (the Mammoth needed Druid 4 and the king had 3).
- Strong vs neutrals, weak vs Divine: Divine fights Evil at ×1.5 always, and Evil has nothing in the kit to answer it without a monster province. Fun: Terror is satisfying; the Crusade wall is not.

### Divine
- Used: Consecrate (11 provinces by turn 15; the capital itself cannot be consecrated, it counts as hallowed), Tithes (+33g a turn at the end, a third of its income), Holy defence, Blessed levies (Veterans at the shrine), Proclaim Crusade twice (each conquest consecrated at once: Gromwells, Stormstone, Goldrot), Divine Grace (Percival returned after a Moderate lair killed him), holy sites (Stonemeadow and Lithstone shine: Power 5 with the Crusade point), a hired wandering White 2 hero as a second consecrator.
- Mattered: Tithes and Crusade. The permanent ×1.5 against Evil decided every Evil-Divine battle but two.
- Never found a use for: Hounds of God, Holy wrath (no hero duels happened), Sanctuary, Pilgrim roads, Revelation (no Evil hero came near; the map's holy sites were all found by walking), Last Rites (see probes).
- Strong: the richest realm by far. Weak: nothing in play; the Divine player has to garrison every consecrated province or Evil walks in. Fun: consecrating outward is a clear plan.

### Good
- Used: Shield of the Realm (×1.75 with a garrison: held twice against twice its strength), Light the Beacons (3 Veterans, 6g), The Villages (Aldmarch tax 4 paid 7), Hearts of Oak (the king survived at 1 HP once), 1g garrisons everywhere.
- Mattered: the Shield. Nothing else.
- Never found a use for: Reclaim (never had the strength to attack), Good Works (no Terror or plague near), Watch-fires (saw nothing I did not already see), Knights errant, Loyal hearts.
- Weak: the economy. Wages hit the tax ceiling by turn 5 with 4 provinces; the Beacons' Veterans pushed it over and the king had to disband 5 companies in three turns. Two heroes dead to one Undead thief. The Shield held the capital three times but the Undead simply took the other provinces and starved it. Fun: holding the line twice was the best moment of the game; losing to the wage bill was not.

## The two probes
1. Undead Harvest+Bind loop vs Good's Shield and garrisons: the loop loses. Twice a 12-13 company host of Crack and Elite skeletal companies (strength 210-237, ×1.6) broke on 5-7 Good companies in a garrisoned province with the king inside (strength 81-148, Shield ×1.75, Tactical 5): 9 of 12 and 8 of 12 attackers lost, no defenders lost. It won only where the king was absent (lone garrisons: margin 90%+). The loop rebuilds a host in two turns, so the Undead still won the war by walking round the Shield.
   Against the Last Rites: not tested. The Undead could not reach consecrated Divine land by land in 16 turns (a sea belt south of Good's land; the road runs 7 hexes through Evil, whom the Undead never met). Indirect evidence: Ironmoor (consecrated, a Divine company died there defending at turn 11) shows 0 graves at the end, while every battlefield consecrated after its battle (Stormstone 5, Thalleaf 3, Goldrot 2) kept its graves. Needs a scenario test.
2. Evil walking into consecrated land vs a Divine player who garrisons: a 1g garrison does not stop it. Gromwells (consecrated, Castiel inside with 4 companies and Proclaimed Crusade ×1.5) fell to 7 Evil companies with no losses and paid 3 promotions. Where the Divine king stood (Reddune, Ironmoor) Evil was repelled. So the promotion is a prize for a province held by less than the main army; the Divine answer is to keep consecrated provinces under the king or behind him.

## Report and order problems
- Diplomacy before turn 6 is dropped silently (the safe start), and so is a declaration on a realm you have not met. A one-line refusal in the report is needed; the rule itself should be in the brief.
- 'spy <province id>' spies the province the hero stands in, whatever the target.
- Garrison company types ('Human Garrison') go straight into the garrison when recruited; a following 'leave_army Human Garrison' then reports 'could not find specified armies'.
- Light the Beacons' line says the Veterans go into the garrisons, but nothing in the next turn's report lists them as new wages until the bill arrives.
- 'Only a king may run down another king' is right, but the chasing hero spends the phase; the brief should say it.
- A Moderate lair killed a Melee 3 hero with the Crown of the Tactician and kept the Crown: lair strength labels need a line on the odds.
- The Undead king's ph4 move was blocked with 'cannot put to sea': the view should mark sea neighbours before the order is written (it does), so my mistake, but a hero order that fails should name the phase it failed in.

## Three balance observations
1. The battle model rewards the defender far beyond the printed strengths: four times a host with 1.1-1.6× the defender's strength lost 7-9 companies to none (Titanheight, Myrhollow, Anvildown, and Divine at Silvermoor). Players will learn never to attack a king on Defend without 2× strength, which makes the game static.
2. Good's economy cannot carry its kit: the Villages give +3 on the cheapest land only, the Shield asks for a garrison in every province, and the Beacons add wages. Good was the only realm whose wages exceeded tax by turn 5. A wage-free garrison or the Beacons' Veterans serving free for 3 turns would fix it.
3. The Undead's skeletal host dies with its king (a necromancer must hold it). One lost duel at turn 14 destroyed seven Elite companies while Lichdom brought the king back empty-handed. Either Lichdom should raise the king where his host stands, or the companies should stand as a garrison until he returns.
