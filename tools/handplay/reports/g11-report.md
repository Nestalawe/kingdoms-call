With ten realms on a 16x11 board, most realms met one neighbour by turn 6 and the map was still half unclaimed at the end: the game was mostly a race against neutral garrisons, and the four wars were two-realm affairs.

## Each alignment, and how the round-24 changes played

**Undead (eliminated).** Living on Skeletal Armies alone is a different realm. Thornax (Necromancy 5 by turn 5) raised 2-3 skeletons a cast from the Coalcrag barrow (3 graves a turn) and the king one; Bind the Bones took them to Crack for 12-20g a turn. The king then declared war on the Druids (instant) and seized their empty capital on turn 6, but the whole 13-company host broke on a Druidic forest defence (8 of 13 lost to none, two hosts that "missed the timing" and attacked separately), and from there every rebuilt host of 3-6 Average skeletons was swept away in turn: capital retaken turn 8, Stormstead 9, the barrow 10, Dreadrot 11, the capital 12. Without Harvest there is no raising from a battlefield after a defeat, the barrow refills only 3, and the spell fizzled twice in ten casts. Bind to Crack was never the limit; gold and graves were. Lichdom needed a barrow and the barrow fell first. Blight the Land (3g) was cast once, on the barrow, the turn before it was lost: the Druids now hold a tax-1 blighted barrow for ever. Verdict: with Harvest gone the Undead is the weakest kit on the table, and it is also the only one that cannot garrison what it takes.

**Druidic (R0 8%, R7 5%).** The forest defence decided the Undead war: three Thunderbirds under a Druid 5 hero, Home of the Wild x1.5 and Lightning Strike put 410-454 strength in a forest against an Undead host of twice the companies. Creature provinces gave a free pack on capture ("the pack submits") in five provinces, and free garrison packs every few turns in four, so R0 ended with beast garrisons it never paid for. The two Druidic realms went opposite ways on the same kit: R0 fought in forest and jungle and never lost; R7's king walked out onto a plain with 5 companies against 6 Crack Evil companies and lost 4, after a Druid-0 hero had lost the grown jungle province Rotscar at -20%. Grow the Wild (8g) made Rotscar a jungle; Evil took it anyway. Call of the Wild worked (2g a pack). Healers' fees paid 2-5g a turn.

**Neutral (R9 won; R2 7%).** Trade Pact, with the round-24 refusals, is still the strongest expansion in the game when it works: R9 bought Ironmeadow (2 Noble Griffons, 22g), Greyhold (26g) and Grashfield (26g); R2 bought six provinces for 11-40g each, including 4 Eagle Riders and 3 Dwarven Guard as garrisons, and ended with 16 garrison companies it never fought for. Neither refusal fired in play: no hero stood in a bought province, and no pact was tried on a realm at war. Ransom finally fired on turn 14 (Encounter order, ransom flag, at war): "Merek spares Requia and holds them to ransom: 9g". But the ambush path (a hero lying in wait from a Defend order) spared her for free twice under the beg-for-mercy rule, the captive sprang from cover and attacked her captor the next turn and escaped, and there is no way in the orders to pay a ransom. Embargo (-20% for 2 turns, 5g) pushed Evil R1 into disbanding for wages: it works against a poor realm. In battle Neutral failed: 13 companies lost to 9 Evil defenders in jungle twice (Savage Fury x1.25, the defender's swing), and the League's gold (306g) had nothing to buy that would change it. R2's Sage 9 Grand Library gave a free discovery a turn; Horse Traders made cavalry 1g.

**Pagan (7%).** Four sites woken by turn 14 (Favour x1.5 cap) and Blood Harvest promotions (Crack to Elite) carried a 5-company host through every wild province it met. The storm at 8g was cast once, on a neighbour at peace, and destroyed and demoted nothing of 4 companies: at 8g a human casts it only at war, which is right. The Wild Hunt refused ("needs a province where Creatures or Monsters still roam"): the beasts next door had already been taken. Rite of the Ley not used (sites in a row). The Pagan never fought another realm.

**Divine (6%, 179g).** Divine Power from consecrations works: Power 3 on turn 3 (a holy site at the capital and one next door), Power 6 (Church Triumphant) by turn 12 with 6 consecrations and 2 holy sites. The gifts still did nothing visible: no Divine hero died, no curse or Terror touched the realm, and Revelation showed nothing a hero had not found. Tithes grew to 15g a turn (7 consecrated); Crusade against the wild x1.5 took every creature province; a hired White 2 wanderer became a second consecrator. The host (10 companies) never met an enemy realm: turned back at a peaceful border on turn 15. Divine's weakness here was the player's: the richest realm after Good and Neutral, with no war to spend it on.

**Good (7%, 286g).** The Beacons muster is now what the fiction says: six Veterans for 12g on turn 12, wage-free, gone home on turn 15 with a line each. Good lit them with no enemy near, which cost 12g and nothing else; with an enemy near it is exactly the right tool. The Villages at tax 5 or less paid on five provinces. The king reached Tactical 6 and Melee 6 by turn 13 from neutral battles alone (Knights errant) and never lost a fight; Good's wealth came from careful blind-free expansion (a Spy 1 hero and no attack on unknown monsters). Shield of the Realm was never tested.

**Evil (R1 5%, R8 5%).** Two different Evils. R1 took a Cave Troll pit on turn 4 and cut Dark Pacts three times (6g and 6 HP each: a troll a time, until "can lead no more beasts" at Druid 2 + 2); with Savage Fury x1.5 and Reign of Fear it took seven provinces, then lost two to the League's walk-ins and held Rotmire three times against 8-13 Neutral companies without a loss. Its economy collapsed under the Embargo and troll wages (7g each): 0g, four companies disbanded in two turns. R8 lost its king to a guardian duel on turn 6, had no monster province (Dark Pact refused: "Mammoths are creatures"), and still won every battle from turn 10 with five Crack companies promoted by Terror victories and a Tactical 4 heir, taking two Druidic provinces and the grown jungle. The altar promotion (one company) never came up: no consecrated province was taken.

## Report and order problems (new)

1. Two hosts moving into the same province in the same phase attack separately unless they "link up" (90% at March 1, roll 97 failed once; the Undead pair "missed the timing" with no roll shown). A player cannot plan around a roll the report does not announce beforehand.
2. A captive gives no orders but Requia "sprang from cover" and attacked her captor the turn after capture, then escaped. Captives should not act, and the ransom cannot be paid from the orders.
3. The ambush from a Defend order ("lies in wait") is a personal encounter that bypasses Ransom; the beg-for-mercy rule spared the loser for nothing, twice.
4. The storm, the Encounter chase and a pursuit all work across a border at peace (the storm struck a realm at peace; the Encounter only stopped at the duel with "not at war").
5. Trade Pact's 13-component price is shown only on refusal; a 40g pact (4 Eagle Riders) is announced after the fact.
6. Dark Pact "needs a monster province" at a Mammoth province: nothing in the view says which Primal kinds are creatures and which monsters.
7. The view never says a realm's Divine Power or Favour multiplier until a rite or consecration line mentions it (the harness now prints Power; the portal should).
8. "The wild answers the druids" put beasts into garrisons the realm's heroes could not lead (Druid 0 heroes), and a Druid-0 hero took -20% on every humanoid company in a jungle the realm had grown.
9. Hire lines print twice; a king's "cannot put to sea" wasted a phase with no sea hex in the order; the Explorer-6 lair spotting and Explorer-8 tax rise are strong and unexplained.
10. Peace needed three offers: an overture is answered only by a Treat in the next orders, and an attack the same turn still lands.

Harness: the orders file gained nothing new this game; the view prints Divine Power (additive, in $H/handplay.ts).

## Three balance observations

1. **The Undead without Harvest cannot hold a war.** Skeletal Armies raises 1-3 a cast from graves that only a held barrow refills; one lost battle costs a turn's host and the enemy keeps the field. Suggest: a necromancer who stands on a battlefield at the end of the turn raises one grave free (the carters' work, without the order), or barrows refill 3 at every barrow held and the spell never fizzles at one; and Lichdom should work at the capital as well as a barrow.
2. **The defender's swing is still the biggest number on the table.** 13 Neutral companies lost 3 and 5 demoted to 9 Evil defenders without a loss; 10 Druidic companies lost 8 to a forest defence without a loss; 5 Druidic companies lost 4 to 6 on a plain. Every realm learned not to attack a held province without a terrain or kit multiplier of its own, so the four wars stalled in two turns each. Not a kit problem: the battle model's loser-pays-everything.
3. **Ten realms on a 16x11 board is a race, not a war.** Half the map was still neutral at turn 16; three realms never met an enemy and the richest (Neutral, Good, Divine) won by buying or taking wild land faster. Suggest a smaller board for 10 (14x9), or more turns, before reading balance from a 16-turn ten-realm game.
