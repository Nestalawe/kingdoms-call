The Human League bought seven provinces and then, in turn 18, walked through the Dwarven League's land into its bare capital. Both Evil realms ended ruined; both lost their capitals to walk-ins.

## Each alignment

**Undead.** All three Undead cross-trained Necromancy together on turn 1 (group practice: all three at Necromancy 3 after one turn), so the spell was never the problem: 2–3 skeletons a successful cast at the capital barrow, about one cast in three fizzling. Morwenna raised fourteen companies by turn 9, mostly from the Druidic dead at the capital. **1g for two skeletons made the realm rich (391g at the end, the second treasury) with nothing to buy**; Bind the Bones (4g to 34g a time) took everything to Crack by turn 13 and then had nothing left to do. War with Good (turn 8) went badly: the Undead lost two big assaults to Good on Defend with Beacons (Aerweald: 8 of 14 lost, none of Good's) and were thrown back again at Hartdrift; they held Grimleaf and their capital (a Druidic king lost 5 of 6 attacking it). Second place on army, fourth on land.

**Divine.** The best story and the worst ending. Consecrate, Tithes (+23–29g a turn), and two Proclaimed Crusades: the first took the Evil R7 capital and four empty provinces, each consecrated by the sword; the Divine king ran down and killed the landless Evil R7 king (turn 12). Divine Power reached 6 (all gifts) on turn 17; the new report line fired seven times ("rises to 1 … Sanctuary", "rises to 5", "falls"). Sanctuary healed once; Absolution lifted Terror nine times. Then Divine's hosts (14–20 companies at ×1.5) broke four times on Evil R3's king holding Greymire (218 against 457 strength: Cave Trolls, Savage Fury, Terror on the attacker), and the Evil king finally took the Divine capital twice. 420g unspent: a capital can raise only a few companies of each kind a turn, even with the pressed levy.

**Neutral (Human, won).** Six Trade Pacts in eight turns (11g to 40g; four of them with 3–4 defenders who became the garrison: twenty garrison companies). The new refusals named their reason every time: "a creature or monster province: beasts take no coin", "another realm's hero stands in Rotmesa", "already the League's". Its king was repelled four times by the same neutral garrison (Telekinetic Storm), but with 300g it raised fifteen companies at the capital in one order (pressed levies of five kinds) and took the Dwarven League's capital with them. **Ransom never came up**: no Neutral hero ever met an enemy hero travelling alone.

**Neutral (Dwarven).** Lost four of five companies on turn 2 to a Veteran Mammoth Host behind "def 3", bought Anvilgreen, Dunsstead and Easthold (11g, 29g, 48g), and took two Evil R3 provinces with a twelve-company host raised in one turn. It kept 383g it never spent, embargoed both rivals, and lost its capital because its army was fighting Evil R3 far to the north.

**Evil (Orcish).** A monster province (Scaleleaf, Cave Trolls) on turn 2: four Dark Pacts at 6g and 7 HP each, Terror everywhere, 16 provinces at its peak, and a defensive doom-stack Divine could not crack. Then three wars at once (Divine, Pagan, Dwarven League), two Embargoes, 0g from turn 18, and the Pagans walked into its bare capital. Bred Cave Trolls at half wages kept it paying until the Embargoes.

**Evil (Dwarven).** No monster province, so **the round-25 Dark Pact from afar was used: "called from afar, at twice the price" (12g, −6 HP), one Cave Troll**. Its war on Divine: walked into consecrated Ironscree (**the altar promotion fired: one company, "the Bolt-Thrower Watch (now Veteran)"**), then stormed the Divine capital, but lost its own capital the same turn and its king on turn 12.

**Pagan.** Repelled twice at the start (down to one company), then steady: stones woken at Dreadhollow (+3 tax), and after turn 13 a walk-in war on the collapsing Evil R3 (capital, Thalwood, Bonevines, Scaleleaf). The storm at 8g: one cast (nothing destroyed, two demoted) and two refusals, one with the good message "the storm falls only on a province holding stones the realm has woken, or one beside such a province". Second place by tax.

**Good.** Villages money (rich all game), Beacons lit five times, all wage-free. **The Beacons decided two battles**: Aerweald (Good lost nothing, the Undead eight of fourteen) and Hartdrift. Its king reached Tactical 8 even with Knights errant capped at 5 (from 7 at turn 7 to 8 by turn 15). Then the Druids took its eastern provinces and its capital while its king was fighting the Undead; peace with both on turn 16. **Bug: Beacons companies picked up into a host never go home** (five in the king's host at the end, the oldest due home on turn 10, all wage-free).

**Druidic (R6).** Forest and jungle, Parley ×1.5, a free Ent at Ruinmire; took Good's east and capital while Good was at war with the Undead. Third place, 16 provinces. **Druidic (R8)** broke its king on the Undead capital (5 of 6 lost), then could not pay 57g of wages and lost three beast companies; peace with the Undead, 0g at the end.

## Report and order problems (new)

1. **Beacons muster companies picked up into a host never go home and never draw wages.** The release step reads garrisons only.
2. **A declaration of war on a realm you have not met is dropped without a word** (the Human League's on Evil R3).
3. **A Defend order rides a king out to a neighbouring province's battle.** Evil R3's king left Stormreach to hold Greymire, and Good's king rode from Aerweald to Hartdrift. Both won, but nothing tells the player that "defend" means "and the neighbours".
4. **Capitals keep falling to walk-ins even with the new sight rule:** Divine's (twice), both Evil capitals, Good's, the Dwarven League's. The sight rule showed the host each time; the owners' kings were simply elsewhere. A "your capital is bare and an enemy host stands next door" line would turn sight into a decision.
5. **The pressed levy is reached with a quantity (e.g. "Shieldwall Levies|3" at a capital)** and works, but a big treasury still cannot become a big army: one extra of each kind a turn. Five realms ended over 300g.
6. **Ransom has no path in a war of hosts:** every enemy hero the Leagues met led armies.
7. Morgrath's "Skeletal Armies fizzles" and Morwenna's successes read well; but a refused Bind ("nothing to bind tighter") never says the cap is Crack.

## Three balance observations

1. **The defender's swing decided every war.** Divine at ×1.5 lost four assaults on one Evil host (218 against 457); the Undead's fourteen Crack skeletons lost to Good on Defend; walk-ins into bare provinces took more land than battles did. Suggestion: show both strengths before a battle, or let a defeated attacker lose at most half its host when it had more than two-thirds of the defender's strength.
2. **Gold piles up and the kits do not spend it.** Undead 391g (1g for two skeletons, nothing else to buy), Divine 420g, Dwarven League 383g. Suggestion: let the pressed levy raise one extra company per kind *per 50g held*, or give each kit one expensive late order (a second Crusade's worth of consecration, a Bind past Crack at 6g a company, a Beacons muster that also raises the capital).
3. **Evil needs its capital more than it needs a doom-stack.** Both Evil realms lost their capitals to walk-ins while their one host fought elsewhere, and both collapsed. Suggestion: an Evil capital keeps one chained beast as a garrison that cannot be led away, or Terror on the capital's neighbours costs an attacker −20% to enter it.
