## 2. By alignment

**Neutral (the League).**
- Mattered: Trade Pacts early (five provinces bought for 18g to 33g by turn 5) and one big merged host late (R0's 27 companies retook four provinces).
- Did not matter: the Embargo, and bought garrisons, which "withdrew" without a fight when R5 attacked.
- The notice (3g, one at a time): it worked as written and felt fair. Both later notices were blocked by an Undead hero stepping in, so no pact sealed after turn 5. The notice's real use was to pin an Undead hero for two turns.
- Ransom: it fired once in twenty turns (turn 13, a level-9 hero, 27g). The Undead treasury was empty, so he stayed a captive for five turns, then deserted unpaid. The League never saw a coin. Its other wins took no one: the loser was a king, or lost under half his host.

**Divine.**
- Mattered: the Crusade against R8 ("the sword consecrates" made four provinces holy at once), Power 6 by turn 10, and Divine Grace, which returned a dead hero.
- Did not matter: Absolution, and a treasury of 70g to 190g it never needed.
- Divine stayed at 12 provinces from turn 10 to turn 20. It attacked R3's king three times with 13, 21 and 27 companies and lost every time to Defend and the Titans.

**Good.**
- Mattered: alliances with Divine and R5, which meant nobody attacked Good all game, and a king with Tactics 7 who broke R3's king at Stardeep with 12 companies.
- Did not matter: its gold (196g unspent).
- Villages at +2/+1: Good is no longer the richest. It was third at turn 10 (77g) and second at turn 20, with less than half of Pagan's gold. Its share of tax (11.5%) fits its 18 provinces.

**Evil.**
- Mattered: the Dark Pact's Titans. R3's king on Defend with three Titan companies won all but one of his big battles, and R3 gained land while at war with two realms.
- Did not matter: Spread Dread late (dread "already grips" the home provinces) and the HP floor, which never came into play: the king rested back to 18 between pacts.
- The wage-free turns: they kept R3 alive but not solvent. R3 made four pacts (turns 6, 9, 12, 15) and held 0g to 10g from turn 7 on. After turn 15 it could not afford a fifth, and on turn 18 it owed 71g of wages on 65.5g and lost four companies. R8 made one pact, over-recruited and was out by turn 11.

**Undead.**
- Mattered: early necromancy training, the barrow next to the capital (taken turn 1), Bind the Bones to Crack, and a surprise war that took the League's capital by turn 10.
- Did not matter: skeleton garrisons. They hold only while a necromancer stands with them, so every lost hero cost a province's whole garrison.
- The collapse took four turns: two storms and Pagan's Defend destroyed the king's host, the ransom removed the best necromancer, wages went unpaid, and both heroes deserted.

**Druidic.**
- Mattered: plain numbers and three heroes each leading a host (R9), and Good's alliance (R5).
- Did not matter: Grow the Wild and druid practice, which changed no battle. R5 ended the game on 0g to 6g.
- R9 over-reached: at war with the Undead, R0 and then Pagan, it had no host left to hold 21 provinces.

**Pagan.**
- Mattered: a Marching-8 king with a Crack and Elite host, Defend, woken stones, and the Storm on turns 15 and 16, which destroyed four companies and demoted three in the Undead king's host just before each battle.
- Did not matter: gold (396g unspent).
- The Storm's limits held: at most 2 destroyed and 4 demoted, and the victim's lines named the companies. The "not two turns running" rule never came up: the Storm only reaches provinces with woken stones or next to them, and the two storms hit different provinces.

## 3. Report and order problems (new)

1. **A declaration of war was dropped with no message.** Good declared on R3 on turn 14. Its king was "turned back at the border … at peace" on turns 15 and 16. The same order sent again on turn 17 worked. Good's report never said the first one failed, or why.
2. **Ransom: the survivors "fall back" into the province being lost.** The line read "What is left of the column (7 companies) falls back leaderless to Dreadflats", which was the battlefield, captured in the same phase. The six skeleton companies then crumbled ("no one was left to command them"). Against the Undead, Ransom destroys the whole host.
3. **An unpaid ransom has no ending.** The captive sat five turns, then "abandons Realm4's service, unpaid and disillusioned" and became a wandering hero guarding a nearby province. Neither side's report said the ransom had lapsed.
4. **The captive's line repeats.** "Wraithe the Cadaverous remains in Bloodscree this season" printed five times a turn (once an order slot) for five turns.
5. **"Only the KING may call down the storm"** arrives only after the turn has run. The order form accepted the Storm for a Sage-8 hero.
6. **One summoned company pinned a 19-company host.** A landless Undead king raised one skeleton company inside Pagan's province. It lost, "stood its ground" (Deathless March), and Pagan's king was "still locked in the unresolved battle … and cannot march away" for the phase.
7. **Bought garrisons do not fight.** "King5 takes Kazpeak as its garrison withdraws!" The companies the League paid 33g for left without a battle, and R0's report did not say why.
8. **A beast company bought by a hero with no Druid skill goes to the garrison.** The warning comes after the gold is spent; the recruit form could say so first.

## 4. Balance observations

1. **Ransom rarely fires and then cannot pay out.** One capture in twenty turns, no gold, and the victim lost a hero for good. *Suggestion:* after the full turn, free the hero for whatever his realm can pay (at least 1g, the rest forgiven); make the survivors fall back to the nearest province he still owns other than the battlefield; and let fallen-back skeletons stand leaderless for one turn.
2. **Evil runs at a loss once the free turns end.** R3 owned 14 provinces and 9.3% of the world's tax, yet held 0g at turn 20 and could not afford the pact after turn 15. The Titans win battles the treasury cannot follow up. *Suggestion:* a Dark Pact company asks half wages from its fourth turn, instead of full wages.
3. **The Storm is free to the realm that least needs help.** At 8g it twice softened the one host that could have stopped Pagan, while Pagan held well over 100g. *Suggestion:* price the Storm at 8g plus 1g for every two provinces the Pagan realm holds (21g at 26 provinces), so it stays cheap for a small realm and costs a leader something.

Outside the kits: **Defend decided almost every large battle** (R3 against Divine, Pagan and R7 against the Undead). Two friendly hosts entering one province still fight one after the other, and even a merged 27 lost to 10 companies with Titans.
