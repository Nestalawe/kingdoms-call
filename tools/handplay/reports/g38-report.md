## 2. Each alignment

**Evil (R0, R7).** What mattered: the king on Defend. From turn 13 to 19 the R0 king, with 4 to 8 companies at mountain Brompass, rode out and beat 12-company Divine and Good hosts five times. What did not: Spread Dread; Divine Absolution lifted the Terror (3 stacks on turn 11, 2 on turn 19). Round 28: the pact line says "asks no wages for 3 turns" every time. The third-of-HP floor never refused a pact: R0 pacted at 13 of 21 HP on turn 19 and dropped to 8 (floor 7). **Evil did not stay solvent.** R0 pacted on turns 6, 8, 12 and 19; from turn 12 its wages (66g) passed its tax (53g) and it lost companies every turn from 13. R7 pacted on turns 5, 8 and 10, paid until turn 17, then lost 7, 5 and 6 companies in the last three turns (60g of that was my own over-recruiting on turn 17).

**Good (R1, R9).** What mattered: the Shield with the king on Defend at the capital. On turn 14 it destroyed 22 of the Undead's 31 companies. The Beacons were lit three times. What did not: R1's gold; boxed in by the Undead and Evil R7, it sat on 10 provinces from turn 10 to 20. Round 28: **Good is still the richest at +2/+1.** R1 held the most gold at turns 10 and 12 (77g, 87g); R9 ends with 192g, the most by 60g.

**Neutral (R2, R8).** R2 could not pay 22g wages on turn 6 and was landless on turn 7 without winning a battle. R8 bought six unclaimed provinces (14g to 33g) and took four by force; it was never at war. What did not matter: Embargo and the Broker; I never found a reason to pay for either. Round 28, **Ransom: never fired.** No League host beat a player's hero, so capture, the held turn, the 3g a level and the companies' fall-back are all untested here. **Pact notice: worked once.** On turn 19 a League hero stood beside Pagan Skulldune (tax 5) and sent envoys; both sides got a clear line with the price (40g). On turn 20 a Pagan hero walked in with her first order and the seal, given third, was refused with the reason. That is fair to the owner. For the League it means a pact only works on land more than two steps from any of the owner's heroes. "One at a time" was not tried.

**Undead (R3).** What mattered: Necromancy on a hero (5 by turn 4, 8 by turn 17) and binding (14 for 28g, 15 for 38g): 29 Crack skeletons by turn 12. What did not: that host. It died at the Good capital in one battle, and the Undead then grew at peace on unclaimed land. The two newest blighted provinces each raised a free skeleton garrison every turn. The barrow was three steps from the capital. No round-28 change touched it.

**Pagan (R4).** What mattered: stones. Repelled three times at Ironflats, it had 5 provinces at turn 10; with three sites woken it reached 15. What did not: **the storm, never cast.** Until turn 16 Pagan never had 8g to spare (my over-recruiting on turn 15 cost it the one attempt), and it was never at war. I missed one fair use: Brokenglade on turn 18, next to woken stones, needed three assaults. The no-repeat rule and the named companies are untested.

**Divine (R5).** What mattered: consecration (Power 4 by turn 10, 5 by turn 11) and two Crusades on Evil R0, each winning one province consecrated at once. What did not: the Crusade's battle bonus against a king on Defend. The Divine king lost 8 of 12 companies three times (turns 13, 16, 19). It ends with 4 companies and 122g it could not spend. No round-28 change.

**Druidic (R6).** What mattered: one hero. The king lost his whole host on turn 3; Cade (Druid 6, Tactical 7) never lost, took the Unicorn province, and from turn 15 walked into ten undefended provinces of bankrupt Evil R7. Creature provinces gave free Green companies twice. What did not: Call of the Wild; I cannot point to a battle it decided. No round-28 change.

## 3. Report and order problems (new only)

1. A 20-company host with March 1 makes one move a turn. The second move order is dropped with no line (Good R1's king, turns 19 and 20). An 8-company March-1 host made two moves the same turns.
2. The League's own notice line gives the sealing price but not the 3g the envoys cost.
3. A League hero can walk into another realm's province at peace and stand there; he must, to send envoys to inland land. The owner gets no line that a foreign hero entered, only the sight entry.
4. The same battle line prints two or three times in one report with nothing to tell the rounds apart ("Battle at Brokenglade: King4's forces defeat …" three times on turn 18).
5. Evil R7's beast-pits gave new monster companies to its garrisons on the same turn the realm disbanded companies for wages. One (Dread Hydras, turn 19) was disbanded unpaid the next turn.
6. The pact line shows the HP paid but not the floor. A player cannot tell how many more pacts the king can make.

## 4. Balance observations

1. **The Dark Pact's free turns hide the bill.** Both Evil realms were bankrupt for the last third of the game, and three or four pacts' wages arrive together. Suggestion: after the three free turns a pact company draws half wages, and the pact line says "wages from turn N: +Xg".
2. **Gold piles up with nothing to buy.** At turn 20 six realms held 61g to 192g (709g between them) while their heroes were at the front and recruiting is only at the capital. The Divine king could not rebuild a 4-company host with 122g. Suggestion: let a hero press one levy a turn, at double price, in any own province with tax 5 or more.
3. **The storm costs too much for the realm that owns it.** Pagan is the slowest starter (3.3% at turn 10) and 8g was a third of its early income, so the storm lost to wages every turn until it no longer mattered. Suggestion: 5g, or free on the turn a stone site is woken.

Play errors of mine: Pagan over-recruited on turn 15, Evil R7 on turn 17, two Good R9 moves went to the wrong province (turns 4 and 10), and the storm was not cast at Brokenglade.
