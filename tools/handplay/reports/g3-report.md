# g3 report — Druidic (Elven) vs Divine (Human) vs Undead (Orcish), seed 303, 16 turns, hand-played as all three

## Result
No throne claimed. **Undead won on every measure**: 18 provinces, 30% of the world tax base, 148g, 49 companies (24 led, 25 garrison). Druidic 7 provinces (17%), 25g, 16 companies. Divine 5 provinces (16%), 47g, 9 companies.

| | Turn 8 | End (turn 16) |
|---|---|---|
| Druidic | 5 prov · 13 led companies · 24g (income 37, wages 35) | 7 prov · 14 led + 2 garrison · 25g (income 45, wages 56) |
| Divine | 5 prov · 7 led · 34g (income 43, wages 26) | 5 prov · 9 led · 47g (income 44, wages 36) |
| Undead | 8 prov · 21 led + ~8 garrison · 21g (income 45, wages 35) | 18 prov · 24 led + 25 garrison · 148g (income 88, wages 62) |

The story: Divine and Druidic met at turn 5 and fought a four-turn war over Faepass, Urdrift, Eaststead and the Dawnshaw holy site; it ended with the Druids holding the holy forest and both realms exhausted, while the Undead, with nobody within reach until turn 14, took a province a turn with two armies and never lost one. The Undead's only two defeats were a 1-in-20 roll at Brammeadow (turn 3) and the last-turn counterattack at Stonescree.

## Druidic — what was used, what mattered
- **Home of the Wild ×1.5 and Grow the Wild (8g)**: decisive. Eaststead, grown from plain to forest at turn 5, became the fortress where Drenn's 12 companies posted 558 strength and threw back two Divine assaults with no loss (turn 10). "Forest Wardens hold the thickets" also silenced every Divine volley. Grow the Wild refused the mountain capital ("only a plains or desert province") — the rule is right but the hint arrived only after a wasted phase.
- **Beast mastery (Druid 4 = 2 + signature +1 + a level earned in battle)**: 4 beasts fought at 241 strength on turn 9; by turn 12 the beast half of the host carried the attacks on Faepass and Dawnshaw.
- **Call of the Wild (4g, twice)**: pulled every wild garrison pack into the king's host — 2 companies each time. Good value; the wild then sent new packs within 3 turns.
- **The beasts are yours (×1.5 storming a creature province, stacking with forest)**: made Brokenwood and Dreadridge cheap on turns 2-3 and brought the Thunderbirds' Lightning Strike along.
- **Healers' fees**: +1 to +5g a turn, every turn a hero sat in a humanoid province; small but it paid a hero's wage.
- **Green healing**: healed Drenn from 5 HP after Seraphine's ambush and King0 after each hunt. Felt right.
- **Druidic king as hero-hunter**: not a kit ability but the game's best moment — King0 (Melee 3→5) ran down and killed Uriel and Seraphine, who both came back by Divine Grace.
- Never used: breeding (I never saw a "bred" line — either the 4-turn cycle never hit a quiet creature province or it is silent), Wild Hunt recruits (4g at Dreadridge/Brokenwood; the free packs made them pointless).
- Weak: wages. Druidic wages equalled or beat income from turn 9 on (56g vs 45g at the end): a 14-company host of 2-4g units on 7 provinces is not sustainable, and the kit gives no money beyond healers' fees.

## Divine — what was used, what mattered
- **Consecrate (5g) + Tithes**: the best early economy in the game: +4g by turn 3, +12g a turn by turn 8 (4 consecrated; income 43g on 5 provinces). Uriel at White 1+1 could consecrate too. The capital is hallowed from the start but the view never says so (a wasted order).
- **Proclaim Crusade (6g)**: refused in the same turn as the war declaration ("none answers"), worked the turn after. Its 3 turns took Faepass ("the sword consecrates") and nothing else; the ×1.5 did not stack with holy defence, so defending consecrated Faepass against the Druidic host (355 vs 130) gained nothing from it.
- **Crusade against the wild ×1.5**: on every turn, because every Druidic host carried a beast; it still lost to forest ×1.5 plus beast mastery.
- **Divine Grace**: Uriel and Seraphine each returned the turn after dying (7-8 HP, at the nearest consecrated province, "Grace abounding" spent no consecration at Power 2). Ranulf too at turn 15. So the hero-hunting cost Divine nothing but a turn — fun for both sides, and it kept the Divine hero count at 5.
- **Last Rites**: fired at Urdrift and Faepass; irrelevant without an Undead neighbour.
- **Holy sites / Divine Power**: Power 2 at turn 4 (Dawnshaw holy site), 0 after Dawnshaw fell at turn 12. Powers 3-6 never came near. Meanwhile the Undead held three holy sites by turn 14 without wanting them.
- Weak: the Divine army. Blessed levies muster as Veterans but Average levies and bowmen recruited in bulk (turn 11-12: 4 a turn at 60g) pushed wages to 44g on 29g income and then died to a Druidic beast host. Hired heroes at 4-5g a turn (Ranulf) were the real wage sink: 11g of hero wages on a 5-province realm.
- Two hired heroes (Sythra for Undead, Ranulf for Divine) died in **duels against wandering guardians** before the battle lines met, and their hosts fought leaderless at −20% and lost. A human would have wanted a stance; the harness has no field, and the brief's default "refuse" costs −20% every battle.

## Undead — what was used, what mattered
- **Harvest the Field (2g a grave)**: the engine of the whole game. 2 companies at the start, 2 a turn from the capital barrow (3 graves refilled every turn), plus every captured barrow (Brammeadow, Scalewood, Magwold, Gorreach, Savagewold, Stonescree all barrows: 7 of 18 provinces): 21 led companies by turn 8 for about 40g. Battlefield graves gave Veteran skeletons of what died there (Titan Warbeasts, a Manticore, Thunderbirds).
- **The dead fear nothing ×1.5 → ×1.7** (+0.05 a blighted province) and +50% fanaticism under the king (+30% under a hero): an Average skeletal company under Grendol fought like a Crack living one. Neutral defenders never had a chance: 15 captures, 1 loss on a 1-in-20 roll.
- **Blight the Land (3g)**: used 4 times (Barridge, Krulmoor, Deepdeep, Moontor): each gave +4g a turn (16g total, more than the lost tax of 5-6g each) and a free skeletal garrison company every turn (25 garrison companies at the end, 1g each). The garrisons cannot be led away, so they are pure defence and wage: by turn 10 wages were 55g of 62g income, and the realm survived only because it kept taking land.
- **Bind the Bones (2g a company)**: used 4 times, 46g in all; the whole royal host was Elite by turn 13. There is no way to choose which companies from the order (target '' bound every skeletal company in the province).
- **Skeletal Armies (Necromancy 1+1 and 2+1)**: fizzled 3 of 6 casts, then raised 5 companies; "no battlefield dead here" at a barrow whose graves it had just spent. Harvest the Field at 2g is strictly better until the graves run out.
- **Barrows**: besides the graves, "+1 Necromancy per barrow" never showed in a report; Wren at Necromancy 2 with 7 barrows still fizzled twice.
- **War without warning**: the Undead declaration of war took effect the same turn ("the dead keep no truce") while the Divine one took a turn: Grendol marched into Stonescree before Druidic could answer. Worth a line in the rulebook.
- Never used: Desecrate (no consecrated province captured), The soul is taken (no Divine hero slain), Lichdom. Living recruits were only bought for Sythra's doomed Blightfield attack.
- Weak: nothing in play. Gold-poor in the middle game (8g at turn 5, 21g at turn 8) but never short; Host-Leader −12g a turn and Orcish war-spoils halved wages on every victory.

## Report and order-format problems
1. A hired hero's acceptance line prints twice in the report (every hire, all three realms).
2. Personal encounters print every roll: Seraphine vs Drenn ran to 60+ lines in both realms' reports; the later encounters printed only "overcome / slain". The first format needs a 3-line summary.
3. Duel stance: no order field; the default refusal costs −20% in every battle a hero leads, and two hired heroes died in forced duels against guardians. Players will ask for it on turn 1.
4. Bind the Bones has no way to pick companies; Call of the Wild takes every garrison pack.
5. "1 grave lie ready" (number agreement); "the The Colossus Steps"; "Realm1's overture of peace to Realm0 went unanswered this year" came the same turn as the overture, before the other side could answer.
6. The view shows a hallowed capital as un-consecrated; Grow the Wild's "plains or desert only" rule is not in the order's prompt.
7. Proclaim Crusade in the same orders as the declaration of war is refused: the player needs to know the declaration lands at turn end (except for the Undead).
8. Defend sends a hero to a neighbour's defence alone if they are the only one on Defend: Nira's 2 thunderbirds met 9 skeletons.

## Three balance observations
1. **Undead barrows plus Blight snowball with no counter in reach.** A fifth of the land is barrows, each a 2g-a-grave recruiting office that refills, and each blight adds a free garrison company a turn and ×0.05 to every skeletal host. By turn 8 the Undead had 3× the companies of either rival for a quarter of the gold. Suggestions: refill a captured barrow more slowly (1 grave a turn, not 3 at the capital and 1-3 elsewhere), or cap Harvest the Field at one grave a turn per province, or make blighted-soil garrisons cost 2g.
2. **Divine's Crusade cannot beat the Druidic forest.** ×1.5 against beasts does not stack with holy defence, while Home of the Wild ×1.5 stacks with Druid-4 beast mastery (+40% a level) and Forest Wardens silence archers: 150 vs 558 at Eaststead with equal company counts. Either let Proclaimed Crusade stack with holy defence on consecrated ground (so a Divine host at least holds what it consecrated), or cap beast mastery under Druidic at +80%.
3. **Hired-hero wages decide the Divine mid-game.** Divine Grace makes heroes cheap to lose but Ranulf at 5g and Seraphine at 2g were 11g of a 29g income on turn 12; Druidic's four heroes cost 7g. A hire's wage should scale with the hirer's provinces or be fixed at 2g for the first three turns, or Divine Grace should also waive a returned hero's wage for a turn.
