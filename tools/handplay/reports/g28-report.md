## Each alignment and its round-26 change

**Undead (R1, sixth).** Two heroes cross-trained Necromancy to 4 to 6 by turn 7 and raised two or three skeletons a cast at three barrows. **Barrow and blight garrisons work.** Skeletons left in the Titanexpanse and Easthold barrows, and in blighted Bardelve, Morscar and Ironbriar, stood without a necromancer turn after turn (Easthold's grew to 16). They became the Undead's reserve: on turn 12 a hero picked up Easthold's 16 and marched them to the front, and on turn 18 the king, back from Lichdom with nothing, picked up Titanexpanse's 11 and had a host again. No enemy attacked a barrow, so they were never tested in defence. The 1g skeleton wage left the realm comfortable (60 to 138g). Bind the Bones took the host to Crack by turn 8 and then cost nothing. What lost the Undead the game was the defender's swing and a duel: 25 Crack skeletons failed three times at the Divine capital, and the king died accepting the Pagan king's duel at Gnaheath, so his 11-company host fought leaderless and was lost.

**Evil (R5, seventh).** Spread Dread and Terror took four provinces by turn 4, a Manticore province was chained on turn 4, and two Dark Pacts followed (turns 5 and 7, every other turn as written). Then wages broke the realm: 40g owed on 38g with three Manticores in the host. **Half wages at home did not save it.** I set all three Manticores into Elderbarrens' garrison on turn 8 and the realm still could not pay. A Manticore and a Basilisk disbanded, and Evil sat at 0 to 3g from turn 7 to turn 20 and never moved again. The pact from afar never arose (it had a monster province).

**Divine (R4, wiped out).** Consecrate and Tithes to five provinces by turn 6, and Divine Power 3 then 4 (one holy site, Grashscar, plus consecrations). **The cap and the one-count for holy sites never bit**: Power never passed 4, and it fell to 1 as the Undead took the consecrated land. The Power line reported each change. A Proclaimed Crusade (×1.75 against the Undead) twice helped the capital hold against 25 Crack skeletons, and nine pressed levies raised in one turn made that possible. But each consecrated province the Undead took was desecrated, and every living Undead company rose a tier (the Undead altar promotion is still the whole column). Divine ran out of gold, lost companies to wages, and the Pagans walked into the weakened capital.

**Good (R6, third).** Steady expansion, the Villages paying on its poor land, and a war that took the last Druid R9 land; the king ran down and killed the second Druid R9 king. Pressed levies raised 12 garrison companies at the capital for gold it had no other use for (408g left). Never attacked, so the Shield and the Beacons never came into play.

R7 woke one site, lost its capital to Neutral R8, and its storm on its own lost capital (8g) destroyed nothing and demoted two companies. **The two-company cap on the storm was never reached**, so it is untested here.

R0 sent envoys to the Druids' Stonehollow and sealed it for 40g next turn; R8 did the same to Pagan R7's Barstone. Both times the owner was told and sent a hero to block in phase 0 of the next turn, and both times the pact was sealed first, so the hero was "turned back at the border, at peace". The double price is nothing to a League with 200 to 900g. **Ransom in the duel before a battle never fired.** R8's king, carrying the ransom plan, challenged the Pagan hero leading R7's capital host; the hero refused ("will not answer the call"), which only costs him ×0.8 morale, so no capture was possible. R8 took the capital by force.

**Druidic (R3 eighth; R9 landless).** R9's kings died twice (Evil, then Good, each by Encounter) and the realm was bankrupt by turn 6. R3 grew Starmeadow into forest and twice held Stonehollow in forest at ×1.5, but its king died accepting the Neutral heir's duel on turn 13.

**The bare-capital warning** never fired: every capital that fell had a garrison or a hero in it when the enemy arrived.

## Report and order problems (new)

1. **The pact notice cannot be acted on.** The pact is sealed in phase 0 of the next turn, before the owner's phase-0 move, so a hero sent to block it always arrives too late. Only a hero already standing there when the envoys arrive can block.
2. **Ransom in a battle duel depends on the target accepting.** A hero who refuses is never captured, and a hero leading a host can always refuse at ×0.8. A League player cannot make a capture happen.
3. **An heir's skills are lost unless sent through kingSkills** (three heirs this game); nothing in the turn report asks for them.
4. **A king who dies in the duel before a battle loses his host for the battle and, for the Undead, for good.** The Undead king's 11 skeletons were beaten leaderless the moment he fell.
5. **The Undead altar promotion is still every company** when it desecrates a consecrated province; Evil's is now one.
6. **Evil's wage line gives no hint which monsters draw half wages.** Three Manticores set down at home still left the realm unable to pay.

## Three balance observations

1. **The League's pact on another realm is unstoppable and too cheap for a rich League.** Twice the owner was warned and could do nothing. Suggest: the pact can be sealed only from phase 2 of the next turn (so a phase-0 or phase-1 move can block it), and the price rises with the League's treasury or is capped at one such pact a turn.
2. **Evil without a large treasury collapses and then sits idle.** Half wages at home were not enough: Evil was at 0g for 13 turns with seven provinces. Suggest monster companies at a flat 3g wage under the Evil kit, or Tribute of Fear paying 2g a province instead of 1g.
3. **Duels decide the big wars.** Three kings died by accepting duels, and the Undead lost its whole host with its king. Suggest kings default to refusing a duel, and a Undead host whose king falls before a battle stands as a garrison in the province until a necromancer takes it up, as the barrow garrisons already do.
