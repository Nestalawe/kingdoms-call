## Each alignment, and how its round-26 change played

**Undead (fifth).** The map made the Undead an island of six provinces with no land neighbour; the king had to buy longships (needing one hull for two land companies, and a king leading ships "cannot march overland" until each one is left behind with its own order). **Skeleton garrisons on barrows and blighted land work:** the Gnascree barrow garrison grew to 25 skeletal companies standing without a necromancer, and blighted Gorvines and Balhollow raised their own. Nobody attacked them, so whether they hold under assault is untested. **The 1g wage bit hard:** those 31 garrison skeletons cost 31g a turn on a 30g tax base, the realm sat at 0 to 9g from turn 13, and twice the shortfall disbanded the longships and the king's living companies ("cannot pay 65g wages"). The barrow refills three graves a turn and the garrison never crumbles, so a necromancer at a barrow builds a payroll faster than the realm earns. Bind the Bones to Crack bit once. The Undead declared on Good R4 (instantly) and took Barfell, Fairmarch and Fangcrag.

**Evil (second).** Three Dark Pacts from afar (12g, −7 HP) on turns 5, 7 and 9 kept a realm with no monster province in the game; then "the pits yield" chained a troll at each monster province it took (Fangtor, Blightgrove) and the pact fell to 6g. **The pact every other turn** was the real limit: six pacts in 20 turns. **Half wages for bred monsters at home** applied only to garrison trolls; the king's host paid full, and the realm still ended with 379g. Evil's surprise war on Pagan R1 (turn 15) walked into R1's empty Starthicket, Dunsfield, capital, Marlreach and Wendscree in three turns. Its first war, on Pagan R8, broke on The Old Stones ×1.6 the turn R8 woke them. The altar promotion never arose (no consecrated land in reach).

**Divine (eighth).** Consecration plus held holy sites took Divine Power to 5 by turn 7 and **the cap of 6** on turn 14; it fell to 4 and rose again as holy sites changed hands, and the report line came every time. Counting holy sites once (only the first shining one counts again) held it at 6 where earlier rounds reached 9 to 14. **Sanctuary healed twice**; Grace brought Gareth back the turn after a Cockatrice killed him. Two Crusades: the first (against Good R9) broke twice at Stoneleaf, 10 Divine companies at ×1.5 against 10 elves in forest; the second (against Good R4) walked into Wyrmmoor and took the Good capital from its garrison ("the sword consecrates"), then lost it the next turn to the Good king's 13 (Last Rites left no graves). Divine ended with 279g it never spent.

**Neutral (R3 first, R7 last).** **The pact on another realm's land with notice** fired twice, both against Neutral R7. Turn 12: envoys at Stonecanopy, R7 told ("unless a hero of yours stands there"); turn 13: R7's hero marched to block and arrived in phase 1, the same phase the pact was sealed, too late. Turn 14 to 15: the same at Reddelve, sealed in phase 0, before R7's hero arrived and before R7's war declaration took effect (a Neutral declaration waits for the end of the turn). The price doubled (22g, 28g), which a League with 200 to 700g never noticed. **Ransom in the battle duel never arose:** a League hero needs an army and a non-king enemy host to challenge; R3's heroes never led armies and every enemy host was led by a king. R7's Embargo cost R3 about 20g a turn for four turns and changed nothing.

**Pagan (R1 fourth, R8 seventh).** R1 woke four sites by turn 7 and ended with 401g; R8 had no stones until turn 7 and was the poorest realm all game. **The storm capped at two destroyed** hit the cap three times: on Evil's capital (2 destroyed, 0 demoted), on Evil's king at R1's lost capital (2 destroyed, 5 demoted, then 2 and 2, then 0 and 3). It softened, it no longer wins alone: R1's king lost three assaults behind it before a second host led by a hero walked in. The Old Stones ×1.6 broke Evil's first war in one battle.

**Good (R9 third, R4 ninth).** R9 never lost a battle: it walked into Divine's empty consecrated holy site and Stoneleaf, then took four Druidic provinces, mostly empty. R4 lit the Beacons three times (12g, 12g, 10g); every muster served without wages and **went home on schedule, including one company from the king's own host** (the round-25 fix works). R4 was attacked by four realms at once, lost seven provinces mostly to walk-ins, and the Shield of the Realm (×1.75) broke Pagan R1's hero once and lost twice. Villages paid on most R4 land.

**Druidic (sixth).** Strong to turn 10 (forest Home of the Wild, 11 provinces), then Good R9's king took four provinces in five turns and the Druid king was left with no army. Grow the Wild turned Scalevale to forest. Its peace offers went unanswered for three turns.

## Report and order problems (new)

1. **The pact's notice still cannot be answered.** The owner's hero, marching in the next turn, arrives in phase 1 at the earliest; the pact is sealed in phase 0 or 1. And a Neutral owner's war declaration, which would block the pact, only takes effect at the end of the turn.
2. **A barrow garrison never crumbles and costs 1g a skeleton**: 25 skeletons there drove the Undead into unpaid wages twice, and the shortfall disbands the realm's *living* companies and longships first.
3. **A king leading ships cannot march overland**, and leave_army 'Orcish Longships' leaves one ship per order, so five ships take five of the king's five orders.
4. **"9 land armies need 5.5 hulls"** appears only on the failed move; the recruit list does not say how many a hull carries.
5. **disband_army on a skeleton** reports "cannot be disbanded as mercenaries … crumble to dust": it works, but reads like a refusal.
6. **Recruiting a type the province does not offer** fails at the order ("not raised in Lithshaw"), with no list beforehand.
7. **The bare-capital warning never fired** in this game, correctly (no capital was ever empty with an enemy host next door the turn before), but Evil's walk-in on R1's capital came from two provinces away in one turn, which the warning cannot catch.

## Three balance observations

1. **Barrows plus skeleton garrisons build a payroll, not an army.** The garrison cannot be led and costs 1g each forever. Suggest the barrow and blighted-land skeletons cost nothing while they stand as a garrison there (the dead of that soil), or the barrow refills only while a necromancer is present.
2. **The Trade Pact's notice needs a phase the owner can use.** Suggest the pact can be sealed only from phase 2 of the next turn, or the notice itself is a turn longer; and a declaration of war by the owner should void standing notices at once.
3. **Late-game peace and gold.** By turn 18 every war had ended in peace and six realms held 200 to 711g with nothing to buy. The kit changes of round 26 did their jobs (storm, Divine Power, Beacons, Dark Pact cadence); what decided the game was buying land at peace (Neutral) and walking into empty provinces at war (Evil, Good R9). A watch company in each province, or a cost to leaving a province empty, would make those walk-ins rarer.
