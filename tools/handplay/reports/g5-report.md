# Game g5: Good (Human) v Druidic (Elven) v Pagan (Orcish), round 23 kits, seed 505, 16 turns

## Result

| | Turn 8 | End (turn 16) |
|---|---|---|
| Good (Realm0) | 6 provinces, 29g, 5 companies with the king + 2 Beacon garrisons, tax 35 v wages 21 | 9 provinces (tax 45, 20% of the world), 30g, 9 with the king + 7 garrisons |
| Druidic (Realm1) | 8 provinces, 70g, 9 led + 4 beast garrisons, tax 52 v wages 32 | **11 provinces (tax 63, 28%)**, 80g, 15 led + 6 garrisons |
| Pagan (Realm2) | 6 provinces, 83g, 7 led + 4 garrisons, tax 50 v wages ~36 | 7 provinces (tax 51, 23%), **176g**, 7 led + 5 garrisons |

The Druids won on land and tax, the Pagans on gold. Nobody came near the Reckoning (45% needed after the price fell on turn 15).
Wars: Druidic declared on Good (turn 10) and Pagan on Druidic (turn 10). Good held its capital under the Shield of the Realm
and seized Stonemeadow; peace followed on turn 13. The Pagans took the Druid capital Mooncanopy for one turn, lost it to Thistle's
Stone Giants, lost Ironpeak, then lost two assaults on it (12 companies gone in three battles); peace on turn 16.

## Good: abilities used and what they did
- **Good Works** (twice): worked, but there was never Terror or plague to clear, so it did nothing. Found no use in a game without Evil or Undead.
- **Light the Beacons** (twice): 2 Veterans on turn 6, 5 on turn 11 for 10g. The Ashwood Veterans made the Shield ×1.75 and won the capital battle. But the seven garrison Veterans then cost 14g a turn for the rest of the game and kept Good at 4–30g with wages above tax on four turns.
- **Shield of the Realm** (once): decisive. 158 under the Shield ×1.75 and home ground beat 554 (eight Druidic companies at Home of the Wild ×1.5); the Druids lost two companies and two tiers, Good none.
- **The Villages**: paid nothing all game. Good's first five conquests were two Manticore provinces (tax 4, creature), a Dwarven mountain of tax 5, and Elven forests of tax 6; the only tax-4 humanoid provinces (Thalcopse, Stonemeadow) came on turns 9 and 12 and the report never showed a Villages line, so I could not confirm it paid even then.
- **Reclaim, Watch-fires**: never applied (Good never lost a province; the Druid king was seen by ordinary adjacency).
- **Call to Arms**: not tried (the brief marks it as the old ability).

## Druidic: abilities used and what they did
- **Home of the Wild / The beasts are yours**: the engine of the game. Every creature province fell on the turn the king arrived (Hydras, Giants, Ents), each time with a free "the pack submits" company, and by turn 7 "the wild answers the druids" was adding a free beast garrison most turns. Attacking Ashwood (forest) at ×1.5 still lost to the Shield.
- **Call of the Wild** (twice): 2 Ents for 4g on turn 5, 2 Giants on turn 12. Cheap and useful; the beasts keep breeding behind.
- **Grow the Wild** (once): Thistle (hired Druid 1 wanderer, Druid 4 after two lucky practices) turned Eldervale plain into forest for 8g on turn 8. Worked as written; mattered little because the fight came elsewhere.
- **Beast mastery**: Thistle's five Giants (Druid 5 with the signature) took the capital back from the Pagan king (296 v 303 base, destroying 6 of 12) and then held Ironpeak with the king (530 v 150). The strongest single thing in the game.
- **Healers' fees**: 1–5g a turn, steady. **Green healing**: the king was always full HP. Both quiet and good.
- Weakness: wages. With 18 companies and the free beasts the Druids ran 67–80g wages against 68–79g tax from turn 13; the free garrisons are not free.

## Pagan: abilities used and what they did
- **Rite of Awakening** (×3, Ferro): +3 tax each, Favour to the ×1.5 cap by turn 6 (4 sites). Simple and strong.
- **Call down the storm** (×6, 4g each): devastating against neutral garrisons (Grimstead: 3 of 4 destroyed before the king arrived; Vakbriar: 1 of 2) and against the Druid king at Ironpeak (3 destroyed, 4 demoted of 10). The best 4g in the game; the only Pagan tool that scales.
- **Rite of the Ley** (×5): the king walked Grimstead→capital→Stormridge→Ironpeak in one turn and attacked. Free, instant, and it let a one-army realm fight on two coasts.
- **Blood Offering** (once): a Goblin Horde for a Berserker +2 tiers. Fine, small.
- **Wild Hunt** (once that worked): two wage-free Veteran Mammoths bound to Ferro, who has no Druid: "Druid shortfall −80%", repelled by one company. Useless to a Pagan hero without Druid.
- **Blood Harvest**: 2g a kill plus promotions: three Elite/Crack Berserkers by turn 12. Good flavour, modest money (about 20g over the game).
- Weakness: the Pagan king lost every battle against a Druidic host in forest, jungle or beside the capital, despite Favour ×1.5 and Stone Circles +30%: 303 v 296 lost 6 companies; 227 v 314; 150 v 530. 176g unspent at the end because there was nothing to spend it on that could lead beasts.

## Strong, weak, fun
- Strong: Druidic beasts with a Druid 4+ hero; Call down the storm; Shield of the Realm with any garrison; the Ley.
- Weak: The Villages (never paid); Good Works (nothing to clear); Wild Hunt for a non-Druid; Pagan Mammoth/Troll/Bear provinces (the king can lead 0 creatures: every recruit goes to a garrison).
- Fun: the storm-then-march turn; Thistle's Giants retaking the capital; the capital holding under the Shield at 158 v 554.

## Report and order problems a human would hit
1. "3 neutral defenders (composition unknown — Spy this province to reveal)" hid three Veteran Titan Warbeasts (177) in a tax-4 plain beside Good's capital; Good had no Spy and lost 2 of 5 companies on turn 2. The hint should at least say the defenders' class (beasts/humanoid) or the rough strength band.
2. **Spy and Steal only act in the hero's own province**, whatever target is written: Ryn "spied on Ashwood" when told to spy Stonemeadow, and "found no suitable target" for Steal with the enemy king next door. The order form accepts a target it ignores.
3. **One rite per province per turn is per province, not per hero**: the Wild Hunt was refused twice because the king's Blood Offering, then his Ley step, had "answered" the stones first. No warning when writing the orders.
4. A hero who cannot lead creatures (Druid 0) is allowed to recruit them: "musters into the garrison instead". Three times for the Pagans; a human would want the recruit screen to say so.
5. "Druid shortfall — King2 cannot fully command 5 units: Forest Wardens −20%" for ordinary Elven companies reads as a bug; it is presumably the command limit, mislabelled.
6. Defend in a neighbouring province sends the hero to reinforce ("breaks camp at once and marches to meet the threat"): decisive at Ironpeak, and nothing in the order form says Defend reaches across a border.
7. The research choice ("choose which to keep in next turn's orders") cannot be sent through this harness, so the engine chose for itself ten times; a portal player would not hit this. (Harness, not game.)
8. A hero's hire line is printed twice in the report; the Beacons' Veterans are reported with "They draw wages from next turn" but the wage bill jump (14g a turn) is only felt later.

## Three balance and design observations
1. **Druidic beast mastery plus creature provinces is above the field.** Free companies on capture, free garrisons every few turns, ×1.5 in half the terrain, and a Druid 4 hero makes Giants worth ~60 each. Three Druidic hosts beat the Pagan king at Favour ×1.5 by 2:1 or more. Suggest the breeding and the "pack submits" company need the Druid hero present (not just the realm), or cap beast mastery at +60% for Druidic as for the rest.
2. **Good's economy abilities do not pay.** The Villages needs tax ≤4 humanoid land, which is the poorest land on the map and rarely next to the capital; the Beacons' Veterans then sink the treasury. Suggest the Villages pays on tax ≤5 (that would have paid on Duncrag from turn 4) and the Beacons' companies draw half wages, or Good gets the Villages money as a flat +1g per humanoid province.
3. **Call down the storm at 4g is too cheap for what it does** (a quarter destroyed, half demoted, on any province beside woken stones, once a turn, no range beyond that): it decided every Pagan conquest and gutted the Druid king's host. Suggest 8g, or make it hit only garrisons and neutral defenders (not a hero-led host), or once every two turns.
