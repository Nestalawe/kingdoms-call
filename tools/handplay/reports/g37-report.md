## 2. By alignment

**Pagan (R0).** What mattered: it was boxed in. From turn 7 it had no unclaimed neighbour, five provinces and 5g. The woken stones let its king's 4 companies repel 14 once. What did not: the storm after turn 6, because it never had 8g again. Round 27: two casts only (turns 5 and 6), destroying 0 and 1 companies, so the caps of 2 destroyed and 4 demoted were never reached. No evidence either way.

**Good (R1, R7).** What mattered: land. R1's two big hosts ate Pagan between turns 12 and 18. What did not: gold (444g and 540g unspent) and R7's army, which attacked R9 twice and was repelled twice. The Beacons were lit three times; the muster arrives as local races, so 15 of the Human king's 20 companies fought at −20% on turn 20, and he was repelled. No round-27 change to Good, but it was the target of both League changes (below).

**Neutral (R2, R8).** R2's king rode out on Defend twice, lost both, and the realm was gone by turn 11 without a battle won, so no ransom. R8 lived by the Trade Pact and by staying out of wars until turn 17.
- *Trade Pact:* three notices, one seal. Bardeep: refused for gold, then refused because the owner's hero had marched in. Vakwaste: sealed on turn 16 for 54g as the third order; the owner had no hero within two moves. Grimwold: refused because the owner's hero stayed all turn. The notice-then-seal rule works and is readable. The seal brought Good's declaration of war a turn later.
- *Ransom:* fired twice, both times on Good's Percival. Turn 18: he attacked Vakwaste with 10, lost 5, was taken; his other 5 scattered. Turn 20: R8's king attacked Boneheight; Percival lost all 4 fresh recruits and was taken again. Each ransom was 9g and was paid within the same turn. These were the League's only two battles won against another realm's hero.

**Divine (R3).** What mattered: Power 6 by turn 9 (three holy sites, eight consecrations), and peace: it grew to 17 provinces without a fight until turn 20, when its king took holy Aelybriar. What did not: anything after the cap. It ends with 688g and 18 Elite companies that fought once. No round-27 change.

**Evil (R4, R9).** What mattered for R4: its king on Defend, who rode into two neighbouring provinces and beat Good's king twice without losing a company. For R9: the sight rule saved its capital on turn 9, then wages broke it (0g from turn 10, companies disbanding each turn). R4's three assaults on R9's capital all failed. Round 27: the Dark Pact floor never applied. The pact takes an 18-HP king to 11, the king heals before the next allowed pact, and half is 9. Only a king wounded in battle could meet it.

**Undead (R5).** What mattered: the barrow. It lay next to the capital, was found on turn 1 and taken on turn 2. Both heroes had Necromancy 5 by turn 4; there were 21 skeletons by turn 6 and 33 companies at the end for 10g of wages. The king's living host lost three assaults on defended mountains; Moldric's skeletons made the late gains, all against empty provinces. What did not: the altar promotion: Divine land was never in reach. The barrow change plays well.

**Druidic (R6).** What mattered: wages. It grew its capital to jungle, sat at 0g from turn 5, held two provinces at turn 10 and was out by turn 16. The jungle capital held once against 13 skeletons. No round-27 change.

## 3. Report and order problems (new)

1. **Ransom is paid before anyone can act.** "⛓ Ransom …" and "💰 The ransom of 9g is paid" appear in the same turn's report. The payer is not asked, and the captor never holds the captive. The capture is a 9g fee, not an event.
2. **The freed hero stands in the captor's province.** "Percival the Noble stands in Boneheight again", the province his captor had just taken, with the captor's host beside him and no companies of his own.
3. **"What is left of the column scatters"** is printed when nothing is left (4 of 4 lost). On turn 18 it removed 5 surviving Elite companies, but the losses line said only "5 of 10 units lost". The report never says the survivors are gone for good.
4. **The ransom price is unexplained.** It was 9g for a hero who led 10 Elite companies and 9g for the same hero with 4 raw recruits.
5. **"King3's forces destroy King9's army and seize Aelybriar"**, while the losses line says "King9: no units lost" and the next says he retreats. The same battle is called "pyrrhic" for a winner who lost 3 of 18.
6. **A landless realm still gets a wage warning.** R0, with no province and no company, reads "cannot pay 3g wages" every turn. No report line tells a realm, or its neighbours, that it is out.
7. **"Druid shortfall" names the Beacons' muster.** After lighting the Beacons, Good's king read "Druid shortfall — cannot fully command 15 units". Nothing in the Beacons line warns that the shires' troops are of other races and fight at −20% under him.
8. **A pact notice does not say what answers it.** The owner reads the notice, but not that a hero standing in the province blocks the seal. I knew only from the kit notes.

## 4. Balance

1. **A host on Defend that rides in wins almost everything.** From turn 9, attackers lost nearly every contested battle: the Undead king three times, R4 three times at R9's capital, Good's king twice, R7's king twice. A hero with 2 beasts rode in and beat a king's 15. The rider gets the province's ground and its garrison, and the attacker cannot see him coming. *Suggestion:* a host that rides in fights as a relief force: no terrain or fortification bonus, and −20% on the turn it rides.
2. **Gold piles up where it is not needed and is missing where it is.** Three realms end with 444g to 688g; three others sat at 0g for ten turns and lost companies to wages. One recruit order a turn and one battle a turn leave a rich realm nothing to buy. *Suggestion:* let a realm pay double to recruit a second time in a province in one turn, and let a king spend 60g to raise a province's standing garrison by one company.
3. **Ransom is too cheap and too fast to matter.** Two captures earned the League 18g against a tax income of 62g a turn; the payer held 400g. The real loss was the scattered companies, which the rule does not present as the price. *Suggestion:* price it at 5g for each company the hero led into the battle, at least 30g; hold the captive until the owner pays by order (or for three turns); free him at his own capital.
