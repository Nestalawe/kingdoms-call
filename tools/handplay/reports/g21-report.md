**Winner: R8 Pagan**, on tax share, without fighting a player: six woken stones, Favour at the cap, and the storm before every assault. R9 Neutral had the most land and bought two provinces from R7. Nobody came near the Reckoning.

The wars: Evil against the Druids (turns 8 to 13: the Druidic capital fell on turn 10 and the realm ended with one province), the Undead against Evil (from turn 9: each took the other's capital, and the Undead host bled away in three failed assaults on Evil-held Morgreen), Divine against R6 and then against Evil and the Undead, R0 against R6 (peace on turn 13), and R3 against R7, the two Good realms (peace on turn 15).

## Each alignment, and how the round-25 change played

**Undead (R5).** The heroes cross-trained Necromancy from turn 1: Sepult reached 4 by turn 6 and cast at 70 to 80%, while the king at 1 trained fizzled about half his casts. Skeletons at 1g for two meant money was never a constraint: 398g at the end with 18 companies. Bind the Bones took the king's host to Crack by turn 11 and was refused on turn 19 ("nothing below Crack"), so the cap bit once and cost nothing. The host of nine Crack skeletons took the Evil capital, then lost most of itself in three assaults on Evil-held Morgreen. The Divine king, under a new Crusade, ran the Undead king down and killed him (Hounds of God). Lichdom raised him at the Khaztor barrow with no army. Desecrate never fired: the one Divine province the Undead took was not truly consecrated.

**Evil (R4).** Turn 3: the Dark Pact with no monster province worked as written (12g, twice the price, −8 HP, Cave Trolls). Once Stormverge was held the price dropped to 6g, the pits bred a Basilisk, and bred Basilisks drew half wages. Evil fought on two fronts and ended fourth on tax: it took the Druidic capital, lost its own capital to the Undead and retook it, and held Morgreen against the Undead king and the Divine king in the same turn. The altar promotion never came up.

**Divine (R1).** The new report line works ("Divine Power rises to 5 ... The faith's gifts: ..."), and so do Sanctuary healing (three times) and Absolution (it lifted Terror four times). Divine Grace returned Rosalind. But three consecrations a point did not slow the ladder, because holy sites carry it: Power 2 on turn 1, 6 on turn 7, 9 on turn 8, 12 on turn 12. Divine also could not spend its money: 955g at the end with two companies. Its heroes stood in provinces that "cannot raise" Human units, and the capital was far from the front.

**Good (R3, R7).** The Villages at +3 or +2 still made both Good realms rich (R7 564g). R7 lit the Beacons twice for 12g each. Both musters served without wages and went home with a line each, but neither fought, because R3 attacked provinces the muster had not reached. Defend held: King7 on Defend rode into Woldvines and threw King3 back. Reclaim retook Starbarrens. With the Knights errant cap, King3 stayed at Tactical 5. King7 still reached 7, through battles fought at home.

**Neutral (R0, R9).** At peace, Trade Pact remains the strongest land grab on the map. R0 bought three R6 provinces (16g, 8g, and 24g for Coalreach, with R6's woken stones), and R9 bought Baltor (20g) and Lornroot (12g) from R7. The new refusals named their reason ("another realm's hero stands in Baltor", "Blightroot is a creature or monster province"). The taken-this-turn rule never came up. Bought land had no garrison, so R6 walked back into two of them two turns later. Embargo was used five times on R6. Ransom was never tested: no Neutral hero fought a duel.

**Pagan (R6, R8).** The storm at 8g was still worth casting every time. R8 cast it three times on neutral garrisons (3+2 demoted, 2+1, 3+1) and took each province at once. R6 cast it five times on enemy hosts (two destroyed of five, then one, two, one and none), and won back Wyrmdeep from the Divine. The Old Stones ×1.6 made R6's elite host nearly unbeatable on its own ground. The Wild Hunt bound two Ents to a king with no Druid (−80%), the known problem.

**Druidic (R2).** The king died on turn 3 in a duel against a wandering guardian (his stance was accept). From turn 8 the realm was at 0g and lost companies to wages. Evil walked in.

## Report and order problems (new)

1. **The sight line names "undefined".** "King9 of Realm9 is in undefined, and leads a modest band" appears when the province is one the realm has never seen. Name it, or say "in a province you have not seen".
2. **Seeing a host is not meeting its realm.** R2 saw Evil's host beside its capital for two turns but could not send diplomacy until Evil attacked.
3. **The sight rule warns but does not stop walk-ins.** Empty provinces still fell every few turns (Starbarrens, Gnashaw, Skullmesa, Coalreach, Eldermesa, Wistreach, Magscree, Alddown), because the attacker was a hero next to an empty province, or a host the defender could not answer in time.
4. **A bought province is reported to its owner as a capture.** "Skullmesa has been captured from you by King0 (Realm0), leading 5 units", when it was bought.
5. **The pressed levy needs separate recruit orders.** "Shieldwall Levies|3" raised one company; three separate orders raised three (the third "a pressed levy, at twice the price").
6. **Divine heroes cannot recruit where they stand.** Orders were refused with "not raised in Narwaste" or "not raised in Fangstone". With 955g in hand there was nothing to buy.
7. **Two enemy kings in one province do nothing.** The Divine and Undead kings shared Runethicket for a turn: "watches King5 pass through but cannot find the opening to strike". Only a Crusade's Hounds of God let the Divine king engage.
8. **A Divine hero's march failed with "cannot put to sea"** on a planned land path, and she died later that turn.
9. **The Last Rites applied on an unconsecrated province through the Church Triumphant, but Desecrate did not.** The two rules count "consecrated" differently.
10. **"practise" is not an order name** ("practice" is). The heroes silently lay in wait instead.

## Three balance observations

1. **Divine Power still overshoots, and Divine cannot spend its money.** Holy sites, not consecrations, drove Power to 12 by turn 12, and the realm ended with 955g and two companies. Suggestion: cap Power at 6. Let a consecrated province of any race muster Blessed levies of the realm's own race. Divine then has a use for its Tithes and fronts it can reinforce.
2. **Gold piles up everywhere, and the pressed levy does not absorb it.** At turn 20 seven realms held over 200g and four over 550g. Suggestion: let a recruit quantity reach the pressed levy, and make the levy one extra company per turn per province of every kind. Or give each kit a costly late order, such as a second Beacons muster or a dearer storm.
3. **A Trade Pact on a neighbour at peace is too cheap.** R0 bought three R6 provinces, including woken stones, for 48g in two turns, and R9 bought two R7 provinces for 32g. Each was empty and lost or contested soon after. Suggestion: double the price of another realm's province, and give its owner one turn's notice ("the League's envoys are in Coalreach") so the owner can garrison it or refuse.
