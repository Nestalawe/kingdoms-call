## 2. By alignment

**Divine.** Divine Power rose 2 → 6 (the cap) by turn 10, with a report line each time; Sanctuary healed a hero who came back by Grace. The Crusade (×1.5) won battles in the open but lost four times to Pagan stones (×1.6) plus Kinship. Gold did not matter: 539g unspent at the end. No round-27 change touched it; Last Rites left no graves at its battles.

**Druidic.** Left alone it reached 13 provinces by turn 11, then had no unclaimed neighbour and stood still for six turns. It only grew again by war on Evil R6 (capital taken turn 18). Its big Warden host was useless in mountains (see 4a). Call of the Wild is the king's alone; a hero's order was refused with a clear line.

**Neutral.** Pacts bought seven provinces early; then it pressed 13 companies, paid wages of 119 on tax of 58, and lost five provinces and (for one turn) its capital to Pagan R5. **Trade Pact, third order:** worked as written. A seal as order 1 was refused; a seal as order 3 was refused twice because the owner had marched heroes in with orders 1 and 2. The cost to the owner was real: Evil R6 bared its capital to block two pacts and got the warning. **Ransom after battle:** fired once, on turn 20. The League king broke two Pagan heroes' hosts; they paid 11g and 8g at once. The gold is small; the scattered columns (R5 fell from 16 companies to 5) are the real prize.

**Good.** R7 won by merging hosts under its king (17, then 28 companies), taking Evil R9's capital, and seizing three empty Pagan provinces on the last turn. R3, an island start, needed five Galleons before it could move (turns 7 to 10), lost Goldtor despite Beacons, and ended by claiming sea provinces. The wage-free Beacons muster (six Veterans) went home on time.

**Undead.** The barrow lay in the capital itself, so skeletons came from turn 1; two heroes at Necromancy 4 to 6 raised three a cast while the king fizzled most casts. Wages ran ahead of income from turn 4 to turn 9. Three attacks on Evil R9's king failed. After peace it grew to 26 Crack skeletons through Bind the Bones, blighted four provinces (free garrisons rose each turn) and an unled garrison held the Khazhollow barrow. **The one-company altar promotion never fired** (see problem 6).

**Pagan.** Old Stones ×1.6 with Kinship ×1.44 made both R8 stone provinces safe from a Crusade host. **The storm's cap worked every time:** eleven storms, never more than 2 destroyed and 4 demoted, each company named in the caster's line. R8 stormed the same enemy host four turns running at 8g a turn (see 4b). R5 grew by plain conquest of the League.

**Evil.** **No Dark Pact was refused for low HP:** both kings pacted at full health (turns 5 and 7), then both realms sat at 0g from turn 8 and could never pact again. R6 lost two dragons to unpaid wages; R9 lost its land on turn 17 and its king to a lair guardian on turn 19. What worked: four companies and a dragon held a mountain capital against 16.

## 3. Report and order problems (new)

1. **A broke realm's captive is never freed.** The League took an Evil R6 hero on turn 8 for 8g. R6 had 0g for the rest of the game, so the hero sat captive for nine turns. Suggest: free after three turns, or let the captor release for nothing.
2. **Ransomed heroes reappear in the province just lost.** On turn 20 both Pagan heroes "stand in Goldmarch again", beside the League king who had just taken Goldmarch. They should return to their capital.
3. **The victim's storm line names no company** ("2 destroyed in all, 4 fallen a tier"), while the caster's line names all six. The victim needs the names more.
4. **The storm's reach is only explained on refusal** ("only on a province holding stones the realm has woken, or one beside such"). Neither side can see which provinces hold woken stones: the Divine king avoided Silvermarch, attacked Silverdown and met ×1.6 there too. The battle line does not name the province's stones.
5. **"Pick up all" took two skeletal Galleons**, and the Undead king's 27-company host then lost a whole turn: four moves refused with the same line four times. Say it once, at pickup.
6. **The Undead altar promotion is out of reach.** It needs a consecrated province captured by a column with living companies. Only Divine consecrates, and the Undead host is skeletons, which never change quality. It fired 0 times in 20 turns.
7. **A Crusade can be proclaimed again the turn after it ends** (turns 15 to 17, then 18 to 20). In effect it is permanent.
8. **"Druid shortfall"** is printed for other-race companies under a hero who is no Druid (Yeoman Longbows under an Elven Pagan hero). The label misleads.
9. **Sea provinces count as provinces.** A hero with three Galleons claimed two in one turn by sailing through. They inflate the province count shown each turn.
10. **A dead dynasty still gives orders.** Evil R9 was "extinguished", yet its two heroes remain, draw wages and can be ordered.
11. **Recruit lists and hull sums are only shown on refusal** ("not raised in X"; "need 5 hulls, only 2 berths"). A pressed levy at twice the price is only named after the order.

## 4. Balance: three observations

**a. Numbers count for almost nothing on a narrow front.** At Westspire (mountain, front of 2) the Druid king's 16 companies scored 248 against 382 for four defenders: nine Wardens stood in the reserve at ×0.125. At Ironfen (forest, front of 4) 13 of 17 stood in reserve. On top of that, fortune overturned a 2.5× edge (a 1-in-81 roll) and a 1.6× edge (1-in-21). *Suggestion:* reserve ranks at ×0.5 and ×0.25 instead of ×0.25 and ×0.125, and no fortune roll may overturn an edge of 2× or more.

**b. The Pagan storm is too cheap to repeat.** For 8g a turn R8 destroyed 2 and demoted 4 companies of the same Divine host four turns running: 8 companies destroyed and 16 demotions for 32g, with no answer. The cap per storm is right; the repeat is the problem. *Suggestion:* the same province cannot be stormed two turns running, or each storm in a row on one realm doubles in price (8g, 16g, 32g).

**c. Evil cannot pay for what the Dark Pact gives.** Both Evil realms were at 0g from turn 8, lost the pacted beasts to wages, and finished last and eliminated. The half-HP floor never came into play, because gold stopped the pacts long before health did. *Suggestion:* a pacted company serves wage-free for its first three turns, and Raiders' Muster pays a little gold (say 5g) with its free company, so a winning Evil realm can keep its monsters.
