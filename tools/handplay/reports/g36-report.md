## 2. The alignments

**Neutral.** The round-27 seal rule worked as written: R0 gave notice on a Divine province, the owner marched a hero in with his second order, and the third-order seal was refused with a clear line. The price was a war and a Crusade. **Ransom after a battle fired once in two chances** (turn 17): a Divine hero attacked with 22 companies, lost 14, and "King0 takes them alive: 13g to the League". The 13g was paid the same turn. The other chance (3 of 7 lost) was correctly under half. Embargo (three times) and cheap dwarves from Guild Halls mattered more than either. R7 was crippled by an early Evil war and lived on 2g mercenaries.

**Pagan.** Seven woken stones carried the realm to second place. Six storms: each destroyed 1 or 2 companies and demoted 2 to 4; the cap of four held twice, and the caster's line named every company. A storm has no cooldown (three turns running on one province, 8g each) and is refused away from woken stones with a clear line. It did not slow the host it hit: King3 lost 2, had 4 demoted, and marched two provinces the same turn. Blood Harvest (2g a kill) did not matter.

**Undead.** Two natural barrows stood one and two steps from the capital, so the code-placed barrow was not needed. Even so the realm was slow: first barrow held on turn 6, the adjacent one on turn 11 at the third try (three Dwarven Guard in mountains beat 6, then 9 companies). It was broke from turn 8 to 10. From turn 11 the 1g skeletons paid off: 14 under the king took the Evil capital on turn 13. Skeleton attacks on defended forest and mountain failed badly (11 of 24 lost; 7 of 14 lost). **The altar promotion was not tested**: the realm never met consecrated land.

**Good.** Nothing in the kit decided the game; a Tactical 8 king with 15 companies did. Beacons, lit once (12g), raised six wage-free companies and sent them home after three turns, on time. R3 ended with 1,014g it could not spend. R8, the second Good realm, was steady and broke all three R9 hosts in one turn.

**Evil.** Strong to turn 12 (15 provinces), then three wars at once. Five Dark Pacts (6g and 40% of HP each). **The round-27 floor worked**: at 7 of 18 HP the pact was refused, "the pact wants strong blood". The king never got back above half, so the pact was closed for the last five turns, exactly when the realm was dying. The pact from a kind the realm no longer bred was also refused, with the kinds listed. Spread Dread laid Terror on neighbours at peace.

**Divine.** Power reached 6 on turn 11 with the Crusade and fell to 5 on turn 14 as consecrated land was lost; a line marked each change. The Crusade took two provinces, then the war turned: the king lost 12 of 23 attacking 16 dwarves on Defend, and the Ransom took the second host. Holy ground (×1.5) saved the capital: 12 levies threw back 26 companies three times.

**Druidic.** R6 was left alone all game and grew from 9 to 15 provinces, fifth. It was held back by gold (9 to 26g until turn 16; wages 49g of 53g income) and by the wild: four of its seven attacks on lair provinces failed, each against 2 to 4 Elder Dragons in mountains. The Call of the Wild (2g) only moved garrison beasts to the king. R9 was attacked by both Good realms; its Defend order rode 19 companies out of their forest to lose 9, and it was out by turn 15.

## 3. Report and order problems (new)

1. **A Ransom destroys the whole column.** "What is left of the column scatters": the hero's 8 surviving companies were removed, on top of the 14 lost. The 13g (1g a skill level) is trivial beside that. The hero then stood in the captor's province with no army.
2. **Ransom fired for a host that rode in on Defend.** King0 was camped next door, answered the incursion, took command and made the capture. That may be right, but the attacker could not have seen it coming.
3. **A hero with no army is ignored.** A Divine hero stood in Dreaddrift while King0 took it and camped beside him: no capture, no line, no move. R9's three heroes stood inside enemy provinces for five turns the same way.
4. **"Crusade: holy ground ×1.5" appeared on turns 16 and 18**, though the Crusade had ended on turn 13. Either the label is wrong or the bonus outlasts the Crusade.
5. **A recruit order gives fewer than asked and does not say why.** Asking for 3 and 3 gave "1× … (1 a pressed levy)". No line names the province's pool. "Not raised in X" does not say what is raised there.
6. **The sight rule uses the rough distance**, so "beside your land" lists hosts two steps away.
7. **Losing a capital brings no line about what it costs.** R5 lost and retook its capital in one turn; R4 and R1 lost theirs for good. None was told what changed.
8. **Two hosts ordered into one province roll to link up** (shown, with odds). When the roll failed, an 8-company king was thrown back by four Great Bears and a 2-company hero then beat the same bears alone.

## 4. Balance observations

1. **Gold has nothing to buy.** At the end R3 held 1,014g, R1 494g, R0 344g and R5 268g. A province's recruit pool caps what any purse can raise, and the pressed levy adds one company. R1 lost its capital with 494g in the treasury. *Suggestion:* let the pressed levy repeat, each further company of a kind at one more multiple of the price (×2, ×3, ×4), so gold turns into companies at a rising cost.

2. **Defence is worth too much, and an empty province is worth nothing.** On Defend: 16 beat 23 without a loss; 12 levies on holy ground beat 26; 7 battered companies beat 15 and then 13; a forest host beat 24 skeletons, killing 11. Wild mountain lairs of 2 to 4 companies beat hosts of 6 to 14 five times. Yet R3 took seven Pagan provinces, the capital and three woken stones among them, in three turns without a battle. *Suggestion:* cap the stacked defensive multipliers (terrain, Defend, holy ground) at ×2 in all, and give every owned province a home guard of one Green company for each 4 tax, so a walk-in costs a phase and a fight.

3. **Nothing checks the leader.** R3 went from 14.4% to 28.2% while second place stayed near 15%. It finished one neighbour, then walked through the next. *Suggestion:* overstretch. Each province above 20 pays 5% less tax, realm-wide cap 40%, shown as a line in the report. A 35-province realm would then collect about as much as a 26-province one.
