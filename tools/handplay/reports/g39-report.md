## 2. The alignments

**Druidic (R0, R8).** What mattered: wild provinces. Both realms kept taking creature land to the last turn with small hosts and no losses, and R0's grown-jungle capital threw back 21 Undead companies without losing one. What did not: storms and rites were not needed. Both ran short of wages early (turns 4, 5, 11): two Veteran Great Bears cost R0 a 29g wage bill. No round-28 change touched them directly; R8 won by taking the Divine capital on turn 13 and then never being attacked.

**Pagan (R1, R7).** What mattered: the storm. R1 used it four times and R7 once; each took 2 companies and dropped 3 or 4 a tier, and it broke Good's attack more cheaply than a battle (Good then lost 10 more companies to unpaid wages). Round 28: the "not two turns running" rule refused the turn-19 storm on Zogdrift with a clear line and allowed it again on turn 20; every victim's report named its own companies. The gap did not weaken the storm: the caster simply attacks on the off turn. R7, the same alignment with no gold after turn 15, did nothing for five turns: the storm is the kit, and it costs money.

**Evil (R2).** What mattered: Dark Pact. Five pacts (turns 6, 9, 14, 16, 20) plus the troll pit gave a 14-company host that never lost a battle after turn 11. Round 28: the three wage-free turns worked and Evil stayed solvent from turn 12 on (39g, 69g, 78g, 55g), after two unpaid turns (7 and 10) that came from ordinary recruiting, not the pact. A sixth pact ordered on turn 18 gave no company; I did not catch its line. The floor: at 7 of 18 HP the pact still went through and left the king at 5, below the third it is meant to protect. What did not matter: Dread; nobody attacked Evil's land until turn 19.

**Neutral, the League (R3, R9).** What mattered: Trade Pacts on unclaimed land early (R3 bought six provinces for 11 to 29g each), Embargo against Good, and walking into empty provinces. R3 ended with 172g and nothing to spend it on. Round 28, pact notice: sent twice at 3g; the owner was told both times with the price, and Evil blocked one by standing a hero in the province. "One at a time" was never tested (the second notice hit a creature province). Round 28, Ransom: **no hero was taken in twenty turns.** League heroes carrying the ransom plan won four battles against enemy heroes; the losers lost 1 of 6, 3 of 10, 2 of 7 and (a king) 6 of 21, so each kept more than half and walked away. So the fairness of the full turn held, the 3g price and the column falling back could not be judged.

**Good (R4).** What mattered: Defend and Beacons. Two hosts on Defend at Zogdrift beat a 24-company assault for 1 loss and 18 promotions. What did not: attack. Three assaults on a League province failed (14 companies lost, none of theirs). Round 28, Villages at +2/+1: Good was not the richest at any point. It held 50g at turn 10 (third) and 84g at turn 17 (third), then could not pay 107g and 92g wage bills on turns 18 and 19. Its land tax (70) is fifth of ten.

**Undead (R5).** What mattered: skeleton hosts are free to keep, so the Undead had the biggest army at turn 10 on 54 tax. What did not: Skeletal Armies fizzled on most casts at Necromancy 3 to 5; the barrow near the capital was never found by exploring; the "dead rise unbidden" garrisons (one a turn late on) did not stop anyone. The Undead attacked a Druid capital, lost its own capital to Pagan R1 for four turns, and ended on 7 provinces. No round-28 change touched it except storms: three storms in four turns killed 6 companies and dropped 8 a tier, all named in its report.

**Divine (R6).** What mattered: the first Crusade (turn 10) took and held a consecrated province against 17 Evil companies. What did not: everything after. The king's host never passed 7 companies, the capital fell on turn 13 to Druid R8 while the king was away crusading, and Evil walked through five empty consecrated provinces. Divine had no province from turn 18. No round-28 change touched it.

## 3. Report and order problems (new)

1. **Ransom gives no reason when it does not happen.** A League hero with the ransom plan wins, the loser retreats, and nothing says why he was not taken (he kept more than half his companies). The League player cannot learn the rule from play.
2. **A realm with no province still collects gold.** Divine, landless, went from 7g to 22g before wages on turn 20. Its report does not say where the 15g came from.
3. **A landless realm gets no line saying so.** Divine's three heroes stood in the conqueror's province for two turns with no order that could do anything and no message about what was left to them.
4. **The Dark Pact floor is checked before the cut, not after.** At 7 of 18 HP the pact was allowed and left the king at 5.
5. **A pact notice on an owned creature province is refused only when sent** ("beasts take no coin"). The province list shows it like any other owned province; the order costs a slot to find out.
6. **Peace offers a turn apart both read "unanswered".** Two realms that each offer peace on following turns never meet; one must answer in the turn straight after the other's offer.
7. **Recruiting does not warn of the wage bill.** Good bought 15 companies with gold in hand and lost 10 of them over two turns to wages it could have been told about at the order.
8. **A lone hero's `pickup_army all` with no garrison there, and a mercenary hire in the wrong phase, fail without a line.**

## 4. Balance observations

1. **Ransom is too hard to trigger.** Zero captures from four eligible wins in twenty turns, because beaten hosts rarely lose half. Suggestion: take the hero when he loses a third or more of his companies, or whenever the League hero wins on Defend.
2. **Walking into empty provinces decided the game.** R3 gained 9 provinces, Evil 6 and Good 4 that way; garrisons cost wages most realms could not pay. Suggestion: give every owned province a free one-company militia that fights only in defence and costs no wages, so a walk-in needs at least a small fight.
3. **The League banks gold it cannot use.** R3 ended at 172g with one recruiting province near the front. Suggestion: let a League hero hire companies in any province held by Trade Pact, at the pressed-levy price.

Also worth a look: the storm at 2 destroyed and 3 or 4 demoted every other turn is still the strongest single order in the game; a cost that rises with each cast on the same realm (say +3g) would slow it without removing it.
