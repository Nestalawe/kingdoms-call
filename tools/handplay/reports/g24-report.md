## Each alignment, and how its round-25 change played

**Undead (out on turn 11).** No barrow anywhere near the Undead start, so Skeletal Armies had graves only from its own battlefields, and the realm never had more than eight companies. Cross-trained heroes did raise skeletons (two Shadow Archers at Lithcopse on turn 5), and Bind the Bones worked (two companies to Veteran for 4g), but the first war, which the Undead itself declared, met Evil's chained Cave Trolls: the Undead king lost at Lithcopse, at Ashmarch and at the capital, and 16 companies bought in one turn as pressed levies (40g) were half destroyed and half disbanded for wages. Skeletons at 1g for two never mattered: the realm had almost none. Blight the Land was cast twice. Lichdom needs a barrow; there was none.

**Evil (second).** The strongest army in the game from turn 2: the king took a Cave Troll province (Dominion ×1.5), chained its trolls, made two Dark Pacts for a bred Troll at 6g each, and never lost a battle under the king in 20 turns. Bred monsters at half wages: Evil never had a wage problem and ended with 484g. The Dark Pact without a monster province was never needed. Terror and looting paid steadily. The altar promotion never came up (Evil never met Divine land). Weak spot: the second host. Wraithe's Defend order rode him from the capital to Yllreach, where he won, but the Neutral king then saw the capital empty and attacked it; Wraithe, back home, destroyed him.

**Pagan (R3 won, R9 last).** R3: four woken stones (+3 tax each, Favour ×1.32), Blood Harvest paying 2 to 8g a battle and promoting the host to Crack and Elite. The storm at 8g was cast once, from stones R3 woke in a captured Good province (Deepvines) on the Good capital next door: 6 companies destroyed, 5 demoted, and the capital fell the same turn. At 8g it was still the decisive order. The Wild Hunt was refused at Aldfield ("needs a province where Creatures or Monsters still roam"). R9 lost its capital to the Druids on turn 7 and lived on one mountain province, where it destroyed the Druidic king's whole 5-company host without loss (defender's swing).

**Divine (sixth, 475g unspent).** Seven consecrations plus one shining holy site gave Power 4 by turn 12; the new report line came every time Power changed and named the gifts (Sanctuary, Grace abounding, Revelation, Pilgrim roads). None of the gifts did anything visible: no Divine hero was wounded on consecrated ground, so the Sanctuary heal never showed. The new sight rule saved the Divine capital on turn 5: the player saw the Pagan king's host beside the empty capital, brought the king home in phase 0, and the Pagan walk-in became a battle on hallowed ground under the Crusade (Pagan lost 3 of 5, Divine none). The Crusade then retook Woldstead and "the sword consecrates" made it holy at once.

**Good (R2 third; R7 broken by Pagan).** The Villages (+3 on tax ≤4, +2 on tax 5) made both Good realms rich (448g and 269g at the end) with nothing to buy. Light the Beacons was lit three times (6 + 4 + 3 Veterans, 30g in all), each muster wage-free and sent home after three turns with a line per shire. Twice in R7's war the musters had just gone home when the Pagan king arrived; the timing matters. Shield of the Realm held Ruindune against Wraithe once. Knights errant stopping at Tactical 5: Good R2's king ended at Tactical 5, R7's at 7 (it still rises, only more slowly). Uriel's 16-company host of pressed levies fought at −20% for want of command and lost 6 to a Thunderbird garrison.

**Druidic (fourth on tax, 0g).** Strong early: Parley ×1.5 and "the pack submits" took the Mammoth and Ent provinces, Grow the Wild turned the desert capital to jungle (8g), the wild sent free beast garrisons every few turns. Then it declared war on Evil, walked into three empty Evil provinces the Evil king could see from next door, and lost 13 companies retaking them. Wages finished it: 80g due on 60g on turn 18, 8 companies (two Ent garrisons among them) disbanded. Free garrisons are not free.

**Neutral (R1 fifth, R8 seventh).** Eight Trade Pacts between them (11g to 37g; each defender stayed as a garrison), two Embargoes on Evil (−20% for 2 turns, 5g). No pact was refused: I never tried one on land a neighbour had just taken, so the new limits went untested in this game. Ransom: the Neutral king, with the ransom flag, Encountered Karrik (an Evil hero alone in his own capital) and got "slips away … cannot close". Then the king attacked the capital he had seen empty and lost his whole host to Wraithe, who had seen him too.

## Report and order problems (new)

1. **A Defend order empties the capital it defends.** Wraithe on Defend in the Evil capital rode out to fight at Yllreach, leaving the capital empty; the order form does not warn that Defend can leave the province bare.
2. **Beacons musters leave on a fixed clock**, not when the danger passes: R7's went home on turns 16 and 18 with a Pagan host still inside its borders. A "the muster stays while an enemy host stands next door" rule, or a line one turn before, would help.
3. **A mutual Treat makes an alliance** (Pagan R3 and Good R7 became "allied" on turn 19), not peace; nothing in either report said so.
4. **Pressed levies let a hero buy far beyond his command**: Uriel bought 16 companies and fought at −20% on all of them. The recruit order should say how many a hero can lead.
5. **A landless Undead king lies in wait in an enemy province for ten turns** ("watches King5 pass … cannot find the opening"): an eliminated realm keeps a king who can never act.
6. **Ransom has no reachable path**: an Encounter on a lone hero inside his own capital "slips away"; the capture never happens.
7. **Recruit lists per province** are still unknown until refused ("not raised in Gashdeep / Goldwells / Bonebarrens").

## Three balance observations

1. **The storm still takes capitals.** One 8g cast from stones woken next door destroyed 6 of a capital's companies and demoted 5, and the capital fell the same phase. Suggest: a storm destroys at most two companies a cast, and a capital (or a province with the king in it) only loses tiers, never companies.
2. **The Undead live or die by a barrow.** With no barrow in reach the realm never raised more than a handful of skeletons and was gone by turn 11; with one (other games) it was a top-three army. Suggest: every Undead capital starts with a barrow (or a barrow within two hexes), so the kit's engine is not a map roll.
3. **Gold has nowhere to go, except into wages that kill.** Six realms ended with 270 to 506g; Druidic, the one that spent, went bankrupt on free beast garrisons. Suggest: free kit garrisons (wild breeding, Raiders' Muster) draw no wages for their first three turns, like the Beacons; and a real gold sink, such as buying a quality tier for a garrison company (Average to Veteran, 5g), so a rich realm can turn gold into defence instead of hoarding it.
