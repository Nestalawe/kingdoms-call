# g30 report: ten realms, round-26 kits, seed 3030, 20 turns

Realms: R0 Good (Dwarven), R1 Druidic (Elven), R2 Evil (Orcish), R3 Divine (Human), R4 Pagan (Human), R5 Undead (Orcish), R6 Neutral (Dwarven), R7 Druidic (Elven), R8 Neutral (Human), R9 Pagan (Elven). Board 16×11. All 20 turns were played, every realm giving orders every turn.

How I played it: turns 1 to 13 every order was written by hand. From turn 5 a helper script of mine (`auto30.py`) proposed each armed hero's best neighbouring target. I reviewed its proposals and overrode them with kit orders, wars, recruiting and the tests below. From turn 16 the routine expansion orders came mostly from that helper, with hand overrides for the wars and kit orders. Treasuries were mostly left unspent, as Toby asked: gold sinks are his.

The cap bound once. The storm no longer wins a province on its own. It softened two successful attacks: R9's on Rotwells, and R4's retaking of Ironexpanse with a host rebuilt from pressed levies.

**Druidic (third and sixth).** R1's king died in a duel on turn 2. The heir, given melee 4 and druid 3, won most of his duels and took 17 provinces. Grow the Wild was cast at the desert capital. R7 was stopped all game by guardian heroes and finished with 388g unspent. Call of the Wild (R7's king, turn 15) produced no visible result. Round 26 did not touch this kit.

**Good (fourth).** The Villages and steady expansion made Good rich (296g). Knights errant held the king at Tactical 5 until late. The Beacons were lit on turn 18: six Veteran companies for 12g, one in each shire, with no wage on the next bill. The game ended before they were due home. Good was never attacked.

**Neutral (fifth and last).** The new Trade Pact notice worked as text but could not be answered in play:
- On turn 9, R6 gave notice on Undead Morcairn.
- The Undead report named the envoys, the price (30g, doubled) and the way to block: "unless a hero of yours stands there".
- On turn 10 the Undead ordered a hero into Morcairn in the first phase, but the pact was sealed first and Morcairn was sold.

Every refusal named a reason. Two of them were "a creature or monster province: beasts take no coin" (Ruinmoor, Blightwells). The at-war refusal never came up: refusals check the creature rule first, and the at-war pact I tried was on a creature province.

The Ransom test failed:
- The League king, on 'challenge' with the ransom plan, fought a duel before a battle against an Undead hero who was not a king. The Undead hero broke off the duel and fled, so there was no capture.
- In the next battle he killed the Undead king. Kings cannot be ransomed.
- No capture happened in 20 turns.

Embargo was used on both of R6's enemies. R8 was eaten by Pagan R9.

**Undead (seventh).** The round-26 skeleton garrison held: blighted Eastflats kept four skeletal companies with no necromancer from turn 8 to the end, though nobody attacked it. The skeleton wage at 1g each was affordable (129g to 167g in hand from turn 10).

The king's host, with six pressed recruits from Vane, broke the League king and retook Morcairn on turn 14. On turn 15 the king accepted the League king's challenge and died. Lichdom raised him at "the Bone Field of Talonridge" with 14 HP and no army. His leaderless host then took Woldthicket, and the living companies stayed there as a garrison.

Skeletal Armies fizzled twice from a hero at Necromancy 4 to 5. Bind the Bones never reached its cap: the Undead had too few skeletons.

**Divine (eighth).** Divine Power went 1, 2, 3, from four consecrations and one consecrated holy site. Each rise came with the report line naming the gifts: Sanctuary, Grace abounding, Revelation. The cap of 6 was never approached. No gift was seen to do anything. Guardian heroes threw back the Divine king again and again, and Divine ended with six provinces and 245g. A consecration order from a hero standing in a province taken the same turn was refused: "only a province of your own realm can be consecrated".

**Evil (ninth).** On turn 6 Evil declared war on Pagan R4, and the war took effect at once. It walked into empty Ironexpanse, then took Elderdeep. After that Evil sat at 0 to 18g for 14 turns.

Its own pits at Brokenfell bred two Elder Dragons. The every-other-turn Dark Pact was used once (an Elder Dragon, 12g and 9 HP). On turn 18 the bred dragon in its home garrison disbanded for unpaid wages, even at half pay. The one-company altar promotion never came up: Evil had no consecrated land in reach.

**The bare-capital warning never fired**, correctly. No capital was ever left empty with an enemy host next door. The Pagan capital beside Evil's host always had a hero in it.

## Report and order problems (new)

1. **A Trade Pact notice cannot be answered by moving in.** The owner's hero ordered into the province in phase 0 of the sealing turn arrives too late, because the pact is sealed first. The notice should say "a hero of yours standing there at the start of the turn", or the pact should resolve after the first phase's moves.
2. **Refusal reasons are checked in a fixed order.** A pact on a creature province of an enemy realm says "beasts take no coin" and never mentions the war. That is fine for the player, but it means the at-war rule is rarely the one shown.
3. **A leaderless Undead host is told it fights "with undying fanaticism under their Undead king! (+50% strength)"** in the same battle in which the king was cut down.
4. **The pressed levy also works for creature companies.** Pagan R9 recruited two Stone Giants where the limit is one, for 30g, then fought every battle at "Druid shortfall −20%", because its king (Druid 2, then 3) could not command them. The order form does not warn about this.
5. **A unit bred in a monster province's garrison still disbands for unpaid wages** at half pay. Nothing warns the player the turn before.
6. **Kings beat guardian heroes far more often by accepting than by refusing.** Refusing cost five of nine attacks on turn 3. But the first king to accept died on turn 2. The trade-off is never explained.
7. **The harness lets realms' orders persist.** When I forgot two realms on turn 12, they replayed their turn-11 orders. This is a harness quirk, not a game problem.

## Three balance observations

1. **Pagan's Rite of Awakening is the strongest economy on a stones-rich map.** Both Pagan realms finished first and second. Pagan R9 woke five sites for 25g in all, and every site adds 3 tax for good. Suggestion: wake at most three sites for tax, or +2 a site from the fourth.
2. **Evil without a monster province near its capital has no income to fight with.** It sat at 0g from turn 6 to turn 20. Every wage bill disbanded something, including its one bred dragon, and the every-other-turn Dark Pact was unaffordable. Suggestion: let Evil looting pay more (say 1g per company of the beaten host), or let bred monsters at home draw no wage at all.
3. **Ransom still has no path to a capture.** In two rounds of tests, the targets were kings, refused the duel, or broke off and fled. Suggestion: a beaten duellist who tries to flee from a League hero carrying the ransom plan is caught half the time and taken.
