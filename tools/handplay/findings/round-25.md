## The short version

- **Your sight rule works for defenders, but it cannot beat a surprise war.** In five of eight games a realm saw an enemy host next door and saved a capital or a province: it brought the king home, lit the Beacons or raised a pressed levy. It made no difference against Evil and the Undead, whose war starts the turn it is declared, so they walk in at the start of that turn, before the defender can move. Walk-ins into empty provinces still decided many wars.
- **The winners spread out.** Evil, Divine and Pagan won two games each, Good and Neutral one each, and Druidic and the Undead none. Over 20 turns no alignment ran away with the round.
- **Everyone is too rich.** Most realms ended with 300 to 1,260g and nothing worth buying. The pressed levy was used everywhere and was far too small a sink.
- **The Undead are now a mid-table realm.** Cross-trained heroes raise plenty of skeletons. But skeletons cannot hold a garrison, so the land behind the host falls, and they never won.
- **Divine Power still runs away**, now through holy sites and the Crusade more than consecrations: 12 to 14 in three games. The new report line works, and the gifts are finally seen acting, though rarely.
- **One bug found and fixed:** Beacons companies picked up into a host never went home and never drew wages.

## What this is

Round 25 took the suggestions from the round-24 games, leaving out the Necromancy one as Toby asked, and added Toby's new sight rule. It then played them in ten-realm games of TWENTY turns: all seven alignments in every game, plus three more drawn from Good, Druidic, Neutral, Pagan and Evil (never a second Divine or Undead). Every realm in every game was played by hand, turn by turn. Eight games were played to turn 20. The bot matrix (240 games, all seven kits on) was played once more with the round-25 code as a second reading.

"Winner" means the strongest realm at turn 20 on tax share, land and gold. Nobody came near the Reckoning in 20 turns.

## What the games showed, change by change

**The new sight rule.** It changed real fights in five games. In g24 the Divine king saw the Pagan host beside his empty capital and got home in time: the Pagans lost three of five, Divine none. In g20 the Pagans saw the Undead hosts massing and stormed them before they struck. In g19 the Undead saw the Pagan king and held their capital 253 to 248. Good realms lit the Beacons or raised pressed levies when they saw a host coming. Two limits showed in every game. First, Evil's and the Undead's wars start the moment they are declared, so their hosts walk in at the start of the turn before a defender can move (g25 twice, g19 once). Second, seeing a host is no use without a second host to send: most walk-ins were into provinces whose king was elsewhere. Seeing a realm's host also does not count as having met it, so a realm cannot offer that realm peace until it attacks.

**Undead: skeletons at 1g for two.** With heroes cross-trained to Necromancy 3 to 5 in the first turns, as you said a player would, casting was never the problem: two or three skeletons a cast at a barrow, 15 to 26 companies by mid-game. The 1g-for-two wage made the Undead rich instead (339 to 862g unspent in six games). Bind the Bones had every company at Crack by turn 8 to 13 and then had nothing to do. What lost the Undead their games was land: skeletons crumble in a garrison without a necromancer, so the provinces behind the host stand empty. Good walked into eleven of them in g25, where the Undead had led at turn 10. Without a barrow in reach (g24) the realm was wiped out by turn 11.

**Evil: the Dark Pact from afar and half-wage bred monsters.** The pact from afar worked as written in four games (Cave Trolls at 12g). A realm that had to pay double for ten turns stayed poor, which is about right. But an Evil realm with a monster province now runs away: a normal-price pact every turn plus half-wage bred monsters gave g23's Evil 34 provinces and 1,263g, and g19's 557g and the best army. The one-company altar promotion fired in three games and is now a modest bonus.

**Divine: three consecrations a point, the report line, and the gifts.** The report line works: it appeared every time Power changed, in every game. The count did not slow the ladder, because holy sites carry it. A held holy site counts one, a consecrated one counts two, and a Crusade adds one, so Power reached 12 to 14 in three games while consecrations added only two to four. The gifts were seen acting for the first time: Sanctuary healed heroes in two games, and Absolution lifted Terror four times in g21. Divine won two games on Tithes and two Crusades whose conquests were consecrated as they fell. But most Divine realms ended with 400 to 955g they could not spend, because its heroes could not recruit in conquered provinces of another race.

**Good: Villages +3/+2, Knights errant to Tactical 5, and the Beacons.** Good was never wiped out and ended rich in every game, so the Villages are still generous. Knights errant now stops doubling at 5, but Good kings still reached Tactical 7 or 8 from ordinary battle experience, which seems a fair ceiling. The Beacons were lit in every game. Each muster served wage-free and went home on schedule, and in two games it decided a defence: in g26 the Undead lost eight of fourteen companies to a muster, and Good lost none. The bug: a muster company picked up into a host stayed there for good, unpaid. The release step now covers hosts too.

**Neutral: the new pact limits and refusal reasons.** Every refusal now names its reason ("a creature or monster province: beasts take no coin", "another realm's hero stands there"). The war and taken-recently limits rarely came up, because the League bought from neighbours at peace. Two games found such pacts too cheap: three provinces with woken stones for 48g in two turns, each empty and soon lost or fought over. Ransom fired once (g23: a hero held for 4g), and then the captor's second Encounter order the same turn killed the captive. Against a hero leading armies an Encounter becomes an ordinary battle, so Ransom still has almost no path in play.

**The pressed levy.** Every rich realm used it: second hosts in five realms in g22, and four pressed levies that saved the Good capital in g20. But it is one company of a kind per province per turn, and treasuries still reached 300 to 1,260g. It also lets a hero buy more companies than he can command, and the extra companies then fight at a penalty.

## What each alignment looked like

**Evil (two wins).** Terror and a monster province by turn 3 or 4 make the best army on the board. Bred monsters at half wages and a pact every turn then make the richest treasury too. Without a monster province, the pact from afar keeps it alive but poor. Three Evil realms were wiped out or nearly so, each after losing its capital.

**Pagan (two wins).** Woken stones, Favour at the cap and the storm. The storm at 8g was cast far less often but was still decisive: six companies destroyed and five demoted in one cast at a Good capital (g24), and about eighteen Undead companies destroyed over five casts (g19). The Old Stones ×1.6 made a Pagan on its own ground nearly unbeatable. The Wild Hunt is still refused or useless more often than not.

**Divine (two wins, one wiped out).** See above. The Crusade, "the sword consecrates" and Tithes now win games. The realm's weakness is that it holds what it consecrates only while its king is near, and it cannot turn its money into armies away from Human land.

**Good (one win, never wiped out).** Rich, safe, and with the Beacons now a real defensive tool. Its one win (g25) came from walking into Undead land the skeletons could not hold.

**Neutral (one win, four realms destroyed).** Buys its way up at peace, researches fast with a Sage, and loses every real war. It was the realm most often wiped out.

**Druidic (no wins).** Forest and jungle defence threw back every Undead and Evil assault it met (g22: Grow the Wild turned its capital to forest the turn before the attack). But Druidic wages ran above income in most games, and two Druidic realms were destroyed or left with one province (g21, g25); in g21 the king had died in a duel on turn 3.

**Undead (no wins).** See above: a big, cheap army and no way to hold land. In g21 a Crusade ran the Undead king down and killed him, and Lichdom brought him back with no army.

## Problems a player would hit (new in round 25)

1. **A surprise war beats the sight warning.** Evil and Undead declarations take effect at once, so their hosts walk in at the start of the turn, before the defender who saw them can move.
2. **Beacons companies picked up into a host never went home and never drew wages.** Fixed in round 25.
3. **A sighting in an unscouted province read "is in undefined"**, and a new Divine realm was told its Power "falls to 0". Both fixed in round 25.
4. **Seeing a realm's host does not count as meeting it**, so diplomacy with that realm is impossible until it attacks.
5. **Recruiting in a conquered province of another race fails for every type asked**, with no list shown first. This is why Divine, Good and Neutral could not spend their gold.
6. **A bought province is reported to its owner as "captured from you"**, not bought.
7. **An Encounter between kings ends in a duel to the death with no warning of the odds.** Three kings died this way. A captor's second Encounter order kills his own captive, and a fleeing hero escapes before capture is rolled.
8. **Skeletons left in a garrison crumble at the end of the turn**, with no warning when the order to leave them is given.
9. **A king on Defend in his capital rides out to a neighbour's battle**, leaving the capital empty. And when a king is thrown back, his later moves that turn start from where he retreated to, which sent one Undead king into a Druidic capital.
10. **A mutual Treat made an alliance in one game and a peace in another.** An overture still reads "went unanswered" the turn it is sent. A "contest lapses: no longer at war" line appeared for two realms never at war.
11. **The pressed levy lets a hero buy beyond his command**, and the extra companies then fight at −20% ("Druid shortfall", the old mislabel).
12. **An unknown order word is silently ignored** ("practise" for "practice"), and a hero then lies in wait instead. The "lies in wait" line repeats every turn for every lone hero, which clutters the report.
13. **Grand Design (Sage 9) has no order name** a player can type.

## What I would change next (round 26)

1. **Give the defender one phase against a surprise war.** Evil's and the Undead's war still starts the turn it is declared, but its hosts may not attack in the first phase of that turn. The sight rule then gives every realm a chance to answer a host it has seen.
2. **Let the Undead hold land.** Skeletons may stand as a garrison in a barrow or a blighted province without a necromancer (the dead of that soil). Put the skeleton wage back to 1g each, because the Undead now end games with 340 to 860g unspent.
3. **Slow the Evil treasury.** One Dark Pact every two turns, and the half wage only for bred monsters standing in the garrison of the province that bred them. Keep the pact from afar at double price.
4. **Cap Divine Power at 6**, and count each holy site once, plus one for the first consecrated one only. Let a consecrated province of any race raise Blessed levies of the realm's own race, so Divine can spend its Tithes where it fights.
5. **A real gold sink for every realm.** Let gold drill a garrison company up a quality tier (say 3g a tier, garrisons only), and add a second pressed levy at three times the price. Show each province's recruit list before an order is written.
6. **Cap the storm at two companies destroyed a cast.** Keep 8g and the demotions. It is still the strongest single order in most games it appears in.
7. **Price a pact on a neighbour at peace at twice the price, with one turn's notice to its owner.** Unclaimed land stays as it is.
8. **Give Ransom a path that happens in play:** a hero beaten in a battle the League wins, and not killed, is taken for ransom when the League's leading hero carries the ransom plan. A captor's own orders can never kill his captive.
9. **Warn the owner when his capital is bare and an enemy host stands next door.** The sight rule shows the host. The warning ties it to the empty capital the player forgot.
10. **The defender's swing** still decided most wars (an attacker at 1.5 times the strength losing most of its host while the defender loses nothing). This is a battle-model question, not a kit change, and I'd treat it as a separate piece of work.
11. **Knights errant is right now**: kings level off at Tactical 7 or 8. **The Beacons are right now.** Leave both.
