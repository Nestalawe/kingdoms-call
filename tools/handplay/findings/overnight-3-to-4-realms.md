## What this is

Overnight I played the round-23 kits by hand instead of leaving them to the bots. In each game I played every realm myself, turn by turn, for 16 turns: reading each realm's report, writing five orders a hero, choosing diplomacy and duel stances, and using the kit abilities the way a player who knows them would. Nine games were played with different alignment mixes (three or four realms each): every alignment appeared four or five times. The appendix has the full report of each game.

How this differs from the bot matrix: a bot uses one ability at a time and never plans a turn around a combination. I stacked orders (storm, then march; harvest, then bind, then march), chased heroes with the king, bought provinces instead of fighting for them, and used each ability as the fiction suggests. Two cautions. One player playing all sides cannot bluff or be surprised, so diplomacy is tamer than in a real game. And 16 turns is a short game: the Reckoning was never reached, so "won" below means the strongest realm at the end on land, tax share and gold.

## What each alignment looked like in a player's hands

**Undead: the strongest kit on the table, by a distance.** The bots rank the Undead last (14% of wins). Played by hand it won three of its four games outright and shared the lead in the fourth, and never lost a province it was defending with the king. In g9 it wiped Divine off the map in five turns: 468 against 181 at Wildpass, then 794 against 141 at the Divine capital, with Divine using every bonus it owns (×1.75 against the Undead, Tactical 4, consecrated ground). Desecrate then blighted all five consecrated provinces, so every consecration Divine had bought paid the Undead. The loop is Harvest the Field after every battle (2g a grave, raising the dead of both sides), Bind the Bones every turn (2g a company, one tier each time), then march under the king at ×1.5 to ×1.7 fear. A barrow refills its graves, so a held barrow is a 6g-a-turn company factory. By turn 8 the Undead had three times the companies of either rival for a quarter of the gold. Everything away from the king is weak (unled skeletal garrisons fight at plain strength), and the host dies with the king: in g6 a lost duel at turn 14 dissolved seven Elite skeletal companies while Lichdom brought the king back empty-handed. The loop also loses to a Good king on Defend behind the Shield (twice in g6: 17 attackers lost, no defenders), but it rebuilds a host in two turns and walks round. The bots never stack the three orders in one turn, which is why the matrix misses it.

**Evil: one doom-stack, fed by Terror and by Divine's empty altars.** Won g1 on every measure. Terror makes every early conquest cheap, Dominion of the Pits and Savage Fury stack on the one host, and "casts down the altars" gave every company a level each time the host walked into an empty consecrated Divine province: five provinces, about thirty free promotions, the whole host Elite by turn 13. In g4 (half the map sea) Evil ran out of land by turn 9 and sank under monster wages (7g a turn a company; Dark Pact's company 10g). In g8 (third of four) Terror took five provinces by turn 6 without a loss, Dominion and Savage Fury took a Manticore pit, and the war on the Druids seized five empty provinces while the Druid king walked into Evil's empty capital. Evil's weak spots are a second host (loses to a three-company garrison), upkeep, heroes dying in duels (a Melee 0 necromancer accepted twice; his eight skeletal companies crumbled with him), and a map without a monster province: in g6 Dark Pact, Savage Fury and the breeding pits never came into play, and the Divine king's permanent ×1.5 beat Evil in every battle but two. Its conquests are as empty as Divine's.

**Pagan: the map decides.** Won g4 (six ritual stones in eight provinces: Favour capped at ×1.5 by turn 9 for 5g a site), came second in g5 with the most gold, third in g9, last in g1 where it over-hired heroes and never had money, and last in g8 where two early repulses left it at 3g with wages above tax until turn 6 and the League then bought three of its five stones provinces. The Old Stones ×1.6 held Titanweald against twelve companies; a Pagan with its stones woken has no reason to attack, and loses land only to gold. Call down the storm was the best 4g in every game it appeared in: three companies destroyed at Hartheight and the province taken without a battle; sixteen Divine companies destroyed over seven casts in g1; the Druid king's host gutted in g5. The Rite of the Ley let one host fight on two coasts. The Pagan king lost every open-field attack on a Druidic host, a Divine host on consecrated ground, or a capital (237 at Favour ×1.5 lost to 143 on home ground ×1.5 in g9); the kit is defence of its stones, the storm, and gold it often cannot spend.

**Druidic: beasts with a Druid 4 hero beat anything in forest.** Won g5 on land, second in g3, g7 and g8 (in g7 it won every forest or jungle fight by three to one or more and lost its king in a duel in the plain; in g8 it took nine wild provinces and then lost its capital to an Evil walk-in because nothing in the kit garrisons). Home of the Wild ×1.5, "the beasts are yours" ×1.5 storming a creature province, free "the pack submits" companies on capture, free beast garrisons most turns, and beast mastery at Druid 4 or 5: Thistle's five Giants retook the capital from the Pagan king and then held Ironpeak at 530 against 150. Grow the Wild turned a plain into the fortress that threw back two Divine assaults. The weakness is wages: 15 to 18 companies of 2 to 4g units on 7 to 11 provinces ran at or above income from turn 9 in both games; the kit gives no money beyond healers' fees.

**Divine: the best early economy, the worst at holding what it builds.** Second in g1, third in g3, wiped out in g9, level first in g6 with the biggest treasury of the night (291g, tithes 33g a turn from 11 consecrations, and the permanent ×1.5 against Evil deciding every Evil battle but two). Consecrate plus Tithes gave Divine the biggest treasury from turn 5 to turn 10 in g1, and +12g a turn by turn 8 in g3. The king under Crusade is a fine duellist (three heroes killed in g1), and Divine Grace brought dead heroes back the next turn in g3. But Divine Power stayed at 0 or 2 in g1 and g3 (it counts holy sites, which Divine rarely held, not consecrated provinces), so none of the six gifts appeared in 32 turns; in g6 two holy sites found by walking took it to Power 5, and even then Sanctuary, Pilgrim Roads and Revelation never came into play; in g9 Power stayed 0 again. Hounds of God never reached a duel: a hero cannot run down a king, and the sentries of a host turn a lone challenger away; Crusade ×1.5 against beasts does not stack with holy defence, so Divine lost to the Druidic forest at 150 against 558; and an empty consecrated province is a free promotion for Evil. Hired heroes at 4 to 5g a turn were a third of a five-province income.

**Good: the defensive kit works, the economy kit does not.** Second in g2, third in g5, wiped out by turn 12 in g6, the largest realm in g7 (careful expansion against neutral defenders, the Villages paying +3 on three of four early provinces, and a king whose Tactical rose from 2 to 8 in ten turns from battles alone, with no enemy ever reaching it) (the Shield held the capital three times; the Undead took every other province and starved it). Shield of the Realm ×1.75 with Defend as a standing watch (heroes in neighbouring provinces march in) won the two big defensive battles of the night: Redreach held 228 against 335 with no loss, and the capital held 158 against 554. But The Villages paid nothing in g5 (no tax-4 humanoid land near the capital) and quietly 6 to 9g a turn in g2; Good Works had nothing to clear in either game (no Terror or plague about); and Light the Beacons is a wage trap: 2g a Veteran company once, then about 3g a turn each for ever. Good was the only realm whose wages passed its tax by turn 5 (g6); the second and third Beacons pushed wages above tax in every game, and the Good player disbanded good companies by hand to survive.

**Neutral: money and mercenaries, no answer to a host.** Last in g2, g4 and g7, second in g9 (where nobody attacked it in force until turn 12, and the capital held), and the runaway winner of g8 (467g unspent: Markets plus five researched tax bonuses on a Sage 9 with a guaranteed discovery a turn, and the Trade Pact buying three Pagan provinces, one with a two-company garrison, for 74g in two turns during the war, with no battle). In g7 the Trade Pact turned out to be broken three ways in the prototype: it bought a province with the enemy king's eight-company host standing in it for 28g (only garrison companies count as defenders, never a led host); it bought a province the hero did not stand in; and bought land has no garrison, so the enemy retakes it next turn by stepping out and in. Three provinces changed hands five times that way. These are code holes, not design, and they inflate the bots' Neutral figure; they are listed first under the problems. Trade Pact bought four provinces in g4 and two in g2 without a fight, each with its garrison, so wages rose as fast as tax; most neighbours refused ("not humanoid"). Embargo cost 5g and took 6 to 12g a turn off the strongest rival: the one thing that hurt. Hiring bands one tier up for 1g is a good trick. Ransom never happened: the capture choice is not reachable from the orders a player sends (the merge of a realm's orders keeps only tactics, notes, duel stance and Evil's mode, and drops the ransom flag), so Ulf's duel win over Grendol in g4 and Cerwyn's over Ryn in g7 could only kill. The bots may set the flag directly, so the matrix may be ransoming where no human can. A Neutral Sage researches at double speed (twenty-odd discoveries in g9). Once a Favour host or a fear host arrived, Neutral had nothing: its wages equalled its income and it waited; in every game it sat at 2 to 12g for five to seven turns on the wages of what it bought and hired.

## Abilities that were never used, or did nothing

- Divine: Church Triumphant, Absolution, Revelation, Sanctuary, Pilgrim Roads, Grace abounding (Divine Power never passed 2). Spy where the hero does not stand.
- Good: Call to Arms (not tried), Good Works (nothing to clear in two games), Reclaim and Watch-fires (never applied in g5), The Villages (never paid in g5).
- Druidic: breeding was never seen in a report (either silent or it never fired), Wild Hunt recruits at 4g (the free packs made them pointless).
- Neutral: Ransom (unreachable), Broker with a hero as target (format unclear; 'hire:<id>' worked in g4), Assassinate (needs Spy 9), Trade Pact on another realm's province (refused, though the kit text allows it).
- Pagan: Rite of the Ley in g1 (one woken site only); The ancestors watch (nothing visible when a hero died, in three games); Wild Hunt for a hero without Druid ("Druid shortfall −80%"), and its beasts expire before a two-turn march reaches a fight; The Old Stones (never attacked at its stones in g9); Blood Offering gave one tier, not two.
- Evil: nothing unused; Tribute of Fear paid 1 to 4g a turn.
- Undead: Skeletal Armies (Harvest is cheaper and never fizzles), Desecrate and The soul is taken (no Divine opponent in g2; none reached in g3 or g6), Last Rites against the Undead (never met on consecrated ground; Ironmoor's 0 graves after a death there hints it works, but it needs a scenario test). Lichdom was used once (g6) and returned the king without his host. Blight the Land was never needed in g9: Desecrate blighted five provinces for free.

## Problems a player would hit (reports and orders)

Harness-only, not the portal's fault: no duel stance in the first games (the default refusal costs −20% in every battle the hero leads), no way to pick the research discovery, mercenary hire syntax, leave_army by index. These are fixed or noted for the harness and are not game problems.

Game problems, in the order they cost the most. The biggest first, on its own:

**Trade Pact (prototype code) has three holes, and a fourth by design**: a led host does not count as a defender, so an enemy king's province can be bought from under him; the buying hero need not stand there; the bought province has no garrison, so it is retaken next turn; and in wartime a hero with no army buys an enemy province, garrison included, for 3g a company (g8: three Pagan provinces for 74g in two turns). The first three should be fixed before the next matrix run; the fourth needs a ruling (no pact with a realm the League is at war with, or a price per defender that tracks strength).

1. **War declared by a living realm takes effect at the end of the turn; Evil's and Undead's at once.** Nothing in the orders says so: the same-turn march is "turned back at the border", Proclaim Crusade "none answers". Every realm lost a turn the first time in every game. A declaration before turn 6, or on a realm not yet met, is dropped without a word. One line in the orders or the report would fix all three.
2. **Spy and Steal act only in the hero's own province, whatever target is written.** The order accepts a target it ignores. Three realms wasted Spy orders.
3. **Neutral defenders are "composition unknown" with no hint of class or strength.** Three Veteran Titan Warbeasts in a tax-4 plain cost Good two of five companies on turn 2; three of the repelled attacks in g1 were blind. At least say beasts or humanoid, or a rough strength band.
4. **Ransom cannot be asked for.** The capture choice is a bot-only field; a Neutral player who wins a duel can only kill.
5. **The capital is "hallowed ground already" but is never shown as consecrated**, is not counted in the total, and Blessed levies do not apply there. The Divine player cannot tell what the capital gives, and wastes an order on it.
6. **Light the Beacons does not state its wage tail**; Blight does not say blighted land stays at tax 1 for its conqueror; Grow the Wild does not say plains or desert only until refused; one rite per province per turn is per province, not per hero (the Wild Hunt was refused twice in g5 with no warning). Each cost a turn or a treasury.
7. **A hired hero's acceptance line prints twice** in every game. A personal encounter printed every roll (60 lines) the first time and two lines afterwards. "1 grave lie ready"; "the The Colossus Steps"; "overture of peace went unanswered" in the same turn as the overture.
8. **Lairs show no tier** and cleared sites keep their index in the feature list, so `search 0` revisited a cleared pit; two Evil heroes died to Medium lairs they could not tell from Weak.
9. **Terror shows on sea hexes; sea hexes count as provinces** once a ship passes (Evil's standings line said 9 provinces for 6 land); a wanderer's name was given to a neutral garrison ("Mira the Grounded's forces" for a hero two provinces away for hire).
10. **Defend reaches across a border and rides out at any odds** (a hero on Defend marches into a neighbouring province under attack, alone if nobody else is on Defend; in g7 the Neutral king charged 98 into 412 because a bought province put the enemy "in Neutral land"). A player reads Defend as hold; the order form does not say otherwise.
11. **Bind the Bones and Call of the Wild take every company** in the province with no way to choose; disband by type takes the first match; Trade Pact's success line reaches only the GM log; the Hartheight stones reported "awake already" after two storms.
12. Garrison unit types ("Human Garrison") muster straight into the garrison, so a following leave_army fails; a lair's strength label (Moderate) gives no odds, and one killed a Melee 3 hero and kept his Crown; a failed hero order does not name the phase it failed in.
13. **Recruit takes one company per order** whatever the quantity, and two heroes cannot recruit the same unit type in one province in one turn; a rite and the Ley walk both spend a province's one rite for the turn; the Wild Hunt's "nearby" radius is unstated; disbanding a garrison company takes three orders.
14. Hearts of Oak did not save a Good hero killed by a Weak lair inside Good's own province (either the half chance failed or lair deaths are outside the rule); the recruit list does not mark ships, so a king who buys Ironclads cannot march; orc companies under a dwarf king with Open ranks still get "Druid shortfall −20%"; a dead hero's Melee rose; "blocked: ." with an empty reason.
15. A recruit order's quantity silently caps at the leader's lead limit, and a failed type prints the number as if it were a unit type; "recruits: Cave Trolls" shows for a monster province the Pagan cannot recruit from; a dead necromancer's skeletal garrison vanishes without a line; refusing a duel costs morale and accepting at Melee 0 kills the hero, and nothing explains the trade-off.
16. The recruit list shows a Pagan-only Wild Hunt price to every realm; "Druid shortfall −20%" is printed for ordinary Elven companies at the command limit; the research pick cannot be given in the harness (portal players are fine).

## Balance: what I would change, in order

1. **Put a brake on the Undead loop.** Any one of: Bind the Bones once per company per game (or capped at Crack); Harvest the Field limited to the loser's dead; a captured barrow refills one grave a turn, not three (three only at the capital barrow); blighted-soil garrisons cost 2g. The loop is three multipliers on one host, and it compounds from turn 3.
2. **Call down the storm at 4g is too cheap** in all five games it appeared in (g8: two castings destroyed seven of eleven Neutral companies and demoted five). 8g, or it drops a tier or destroys a company, never both, and never a whole garrison; or once every two turns.
3. **"Casts down the altars" should promote one company, not the whole host**, or only when the province was defended. Five empty consecrated provinces made an Average Evil host all Elite for free, and Tithes make Divine the realm with the most empty consecrated land.
4. **Divine Power should count consecrated provinces** (say two consecrated = one point) or a holy site should be seeded within two hexes of every Divine capital; in three of four Divine games Power never passed 2 and none of the six gifts appeared. Desecrate should not blight a province Divine retakes within five turns, or Last Rites should stop the free garrison for a few turns. And let Proclaimed Crusade stack with holy defence on consecrated ground, so a Divine host can at least hold what it consecrated.
5. **Good's Beacons should be a muster, not a payroll**: wage-free for three turns and then dismissed, or half wages. The Villages should pay on tax ≤5, or as a flat +1g a humanoid province.
6. **Druidic beast mastery plus creature provinces is above the field**: the pack-submits company and the breeding should need the Druid hero present, or beast mastery under Druidic capped at +60% like the rest.
7. **Pagan's Favour scales with the seed's stones, not with play**: cap the Favour sites at three, or tie the cap to provinces held. The Wild Hunt wants an unclaimed creature province beside the stones; the realm's own does not count, and it never worked in g8.
8. **The defender's swing in battle is bigger than the printed strengths say.** Four times in g6 a host with 1.1 to 1.6 times the defender's strength lost 7 to 9 companies to none. Players will learn never to attack a king on Defend without twice the strength, which makes the game static. Worth a look at the battle model, not the kits.
9. **An Undead host should outlive its king.** Lichdom returned the king with nothing; his seven Elite companies dissolved. Raise him where the host stands, or let the companies stand as a garrison until he returns.
10. **Wages decide more than kits do**: monster companies at 7g sink Evil, bought garrisons sink Neutral, hired heroes at 4 to 5g sink Divine, free beasts sink Druidic. Dark Pact's company wage-free for three turns like the Wild Hunt's; pact garrisons at half wages; a hired hero's wage fixed at 2g for its first three turns.

## Against the bot matrix

The matrix (round 23, 240 games, all seven kits on) had Druidic 31%, Pagan 29%, Neutral 24%, Divine 22%, Evil 21%, Good 15%, Undead 14%. The hand-played games agree on Druidic (strong), Pagan (strong on the right map), Good (weak unless left alone) and roughly Evil and Divine. They disagree sharply on the Undead, which went from last to first because a player stacks Harvest, Bind and the march. Neutral is the hardest to read: last three times, once a runaway winner, and its one weapon that works against a player is a Trade Pact with holes in it; its matrix figure should be re-read once those are fixed. The matrix measures what the bots can do with a kit; these games measure what the kit can do. Where they disagree, trust the hand-played result for the ceiling and the matrix for the floor.
