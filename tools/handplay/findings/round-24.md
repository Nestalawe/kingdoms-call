## The short version

- **The Undead without Harvest the Field are no longer the runaway.** Across nine ten-realm games they never won, finished second twice, and were last or wiped out twice. Their opening is now a coin toss on the Skeletal Armies spell, and they need a barrow and a second trained necromancer to get going.
- **Pagan and Neutral now win most often** (Pagan four games, Neutral three, counting one shared). Pagan wins on a stones-rich map without fighting a player; Neutral buys its way up.
- **Evil is all or nothing.** With a monster province it was the best army on the board and won twice; without one it collapsed on wages, and three Evil realms were wiped out or left with one province.
- **The round-24 fixes mostly worked.** The storm at 8g feels right, the Beacons muster is no longer a trap, and the Trade Pact holes are closed. Divine Power now climbs, but too fast, and the player never sees it. Ransom works only against a hero travelling alone.
- **My top suggestions for round 25:** let the barrow count toward the Skeletal Armies cast chance; give Evil a Dark Pact without a monster province; warn a realm when its capital is bare; three consecrations per point of Divine Power; take the Villages back a little.

## What this is

Round 24 took the suggestions from the nine overnight games and Toby's one instruction (no more Harvest the Field), and then played them in ten-realm games: all seven alignments in every game, plus three more drawn from Good, Druidic, Neutral, Pagan and Evil (never a second Divine or Undead). Every realm in every game was played by me, turn by turn, for 16 turns. Nine games were played to the end; the bot matrix (240 games, all seven kits on) was also played once with the round-24 code, as a second reading.

The ten-realm board is 16 by 11 hexes. Nobody meets anyone before turn 6 or 7, so these games have a long opening against neutral land and a short war. "Winner" means the strongest realm at the end on land, tax share and gold; nobody came near the Reckoning in 16 turns.

## What the hand-played games showed, change by change

**Undead without Harvest the Field: slow, dependent on a barrow and a second necromancer, and never the winner.** The Undead opening is now a dice game. Skeletal Armies casts at 30% plus 10% a level of trained Necromancy, and the kit's barrow bonus and signature bonus count for the companies a cast raises but not for the roll (that is how the engine's cast chance is written: it reads trained levels and items only). A king at Necromancy 1 casts at 40%: in g10 he fizzled seven casts in a row and raised his first skeleton on turn 5. Every game that went well for the Undead had a second necromancer trained to 4 or 5 by turn 5, casting at 70 to 80% for two or three companies a time at a held barrow (three graves a turn). From there the old shape returns: 15 to 25 skeletal companies by turn 8 to 11, Bind the Bones to Crack, the king's fear multiplier, and a host that won every battle it fought under the king (g10: six provinces in six turns without a loss; g13: one battle lost in sixteen turns, second place and the richest realm). What is gone is the recovery: with no Harvest there is no raising from a battlefield after a defeat, so one lost battle costs a turn's host (g11: the host broke on a Druidic forest, and the realm was wiped out in six turns). With no barrow in reach (g18) the Undead never got going at all: battlefield graves empty after one cast, half the casts fizzled even at Necromancy 4 or 5, the host never passed seven companies and the realm ended on 0g. In g14 one trained necromancer raised about seventeen companies and the Undead finished level second, until the Druids walked into its empty capital. Hero-led skeletal hosts and Average skeletons still lose to a two-company garrison (g12, four times). In g16 the king fizzled about ten casts in twelve, the host never passed 13 companies, Pagan storms took the capital twice, and the king lost a duel and came back by Lichdom with 9 HP and no army: last of ten. So the Undead is now a one-king realm with a slow start and no second chance, which is a fair place for it; the bots cannot play it at all (5% of matrix wins).

**Two friendly hosts entering one province fight separately.** The Undead lost its turn-7 assault in g15 and its war in g11 that way: the two hosts attacked one after the other, each against the full garrison, on a hidden "link up" roll. Nothing in the order form says so.

**Bind the Bones to Crack rarely bit.** In one game (g17) every company was Crack by turn 9 and later binds were refused, leaving the realm 309g with nothing to buy. In no game did the cap cost the Undead a battle; the king's host was Crack by turn 6 to 12 and won from there. The brake that matters is money, not tiers: a skeleton costs 1g a turn, the Undead has no tax to speak of, and two games had the Undead at 0 to 8g while winning.

**The storm at 8g is still the best order on the Pagan sheet, cast half as often.** Cast three times a game instead of six or seven. It still gutted a host once in every game it was used in (g12: two of the Divine king's eight destroyed and four demoted; g13: one destroyed and three demoted of five), and in g15 three casts over three turns destroyed eight of a capital's nine defenders before the assault, so it is still the siege engine where the Pagan is patient; elsewhere the assault behind it was repelled twice. At 8g a player casts it only at war and only where the stones are near the front; two Pagan realms never cast it, and one cast it eight times in g16 with results from nothing to decisive. That is about right: keep 8g.

**The altar promotion fired once, and is now a bonus, not an engine.** In g14 an Evil host took consecrated Oldhold twice and one company rose each time. In the other games no Evil realm reached consecrated land: Evil's neighbours were rarely Divine, and the Divine capital is hallowed, not consecrated, so it never counts. Note that the Undead's altar promotion is still every company: in g18 an Undead column walked into an empty consecrated province and every living company in it rose a tier.

**Divine Power from consecrations works, and the player cannot see it.** Five consecrations gave Power 2 with no holy site (g10); four plus a Crusade gave Power 3 and Revelation (g12); six consecrations and two holy sites reached Power 6, the Church Triumphant, by turn 12 (g11); eleven consecrations and one holy site reached Power 6 by turn 14 in g16 (all six gifts, none seen to act); eight consecrations and one holy site reached Power 6 in g15, where "the sword consecrates" under a Proclaimed Crusade turned every conquest into a consecrated, tithe-paying province and took Divine from three provinces and 9g at turn 8 to nine provinces and 163g. So the change does what it says. In g17 eight consecrations, two shining holy sites and a Crusade gave Power 9 by turn 13, and in g18 three holy sites and seven consecrations gave Power 10: past the top of the ladder. Divine on Crusade now snowballs: the sword consecrates every conquest, each consecration pays Tithes and counts for Power. Two problems remain. Nothing in a realm's report states its Divine Power or names the gift that came (the hand-play view now prints it; the portal should). And the gifts did nothing visible in nine games: no Divine hero died (Grace abounding), no curse or Terror touched the realm (Absolution), Revelation showed nothing a hero had not found, and Pilgrim Roads never moved a host that had somewhere to go. Divine's game is Tithes, the Crusade multipliers and Holy defence; the Power ladder is flavour until a gift changes a battle.

**The Beacons are no longer a trap, and the Villages pay.** The Beacons were lit in every Good game (three to six Veterans at 2g each), drew no wages, and went home on schedule with a line each. In g17 a muster decided a war: it held Deepsands and destroyed all six companies of an Undead hero's host. The wage trap is gone and a player now lights them before a fight rather than regretting it after. The Villages on tax 5 or less paid +3 on four or five provinces in every Good realm from turn 3, and the two Good realms in g10 and g12 were the richest on the board. In g14 a Good realm won with 17 provinces and the other sat on 586g it could not spend; in g18 the two Good realms ended with 306g and 454g. At tax 5 the Villages pay on most Good land, which is one step too generous. Good's one defeat (g13) was to thirteen Evil companies with six trolls at ×1.5, which the Shield at ×1.75 could not hold; its one war of conquest (g12) was won by a king whose Tactical went from 1 to 9 in battles against neutral garrisons (Knights errant doubling the gain), which is Good's real power now.

**Trade Pact refusals held; the League still buys its way to the top.** The two refusals fired where they should (g13: a province of a realm at war; g12: a province with a hero in it) and the old holes are closed. The League won three games (one shared) and was near the top in most others, by buying: eight pacts in g12 (three of them other realms' provinces, bought from an armyless king or the turn after a neighbour captured them), six in g10, and a 44g buy of a tax-8 province with four defenders in g13. The pact's defenders stand as the garrison, so a Neutral realm ends with fifteen garrison companies it never fought for and 300 to 430g it cannot spend on anything that wins a battle: thirteen League companies lost to nine Evil defenders in jungle twice (g11). The Embargo at 5g is good value against a poor realm (it pushed Evil into disbanding for wages in g11) and nothing against a rich one.

**Ransom now works, but only on a lone hero.** The flag reaches the engine. It fired twice in the round: in g11 a hero was held for 9g (and then sprang from cover and escaped), and in g17 the Neutral king captured a Divine hero for 9g and the Divine realm paid automatically the next turn. Everywhere else no capture came: an Encounter on a hero leading armies becomes an ordinary battle, a target on 'refuse' declines the duel (g14), and nothing in the orders prompts or reports the choice. Ransom needs a path that exists in play: a hero captured after a won battle, or a duel the battle offers.

## What each alignment looked like across the games

**Pagan: the map's stones still decide it, and it wins without fighting.** Won g12 with twenty provinces of neutral land and 662g, shared the top of g10 with two copies, and was wiped out in g13 by the first Undead host at its door. Rite of Awakening at every stones province (Favour at the ×1.5 cap by turn 9 or 10), Blood Harvest making a whole host Elite on neutral kills by turn 12, the Wild Hunt for 4 to 5g (but its beasts expire before a two-turn march reaches a fight), and the Ley. A Pagan with woken stones has no reason to attack a player and every reason to take wild land; the one that attacked a capital (g12, five Elite after a storm) was repelled under Holy defence.

**Neutral: first or second by purchase, last in battle.** See above. Research with a Sage 9 (twenty discoveries, a free one a turn from the Grand Library) adds a second economy: Markets plus five researched tax bonuses made the League the richest realm in two games. Ransom fired twice in the round: in g11 the captive then sprang from cover and escaped; in g17 the Divine realm paid 9g automatically the next turn.

**Good: safe, rich, and a king who becomes the strongest piece on the board.** Good won g14 with 17 provinces. Most Good realms were never attacked and finished near the top on gold; the one in g13 died to Evil's trolls. The kit is now coherent: Villages money, a Beacons muster for a defence, the Shield when it comes. The thing to watch is Knights errant: both Good kings in g12 reached Tactical 9 from battles alone and one of them beat Evil's whole kit five times in six.

**Evil: a monster province or nothing.** Evil won g17 (18 provinces, never a battle lost under the king, a whole Druidic realm taken and its landless king killed on turn 16) and g13 (R0: Cave Trolls chained on turn 4, a Dark Pact every turn, six trolls by turn 12, Savage Fury and Reign of Fear on every battle; it took Good's six provinces without losing a company). Most other Evil realms had no monster province in reach or lost it: Terror and looting took five neutral provinces by turn 7 every time, and then the wage bill for monsters (7g a Titan, 10g a Dark Pact company) or a single lost assault undid the realm (g10: thirteen attacking a mountain capital lost eight; g12: five battles of six lost to a Good king). Of fifteen Evil realms, three were wiped out or left with one province. The altar promotion fired once in nine games (g14).

**Druidic: unbeatable in forest, undone by wages and by walk-ins.** Druidic hosts in forest or jungle at Home of the Wild ×1.5 with a Druid 4 or 5 hero threw back every attack (g11: three Thunderbirds against twice their number of skeletons; g10: a mountain capital against thirteen). On a plain the same host lost its king (g11). Wages ran level with or above tax in every Druidic realm by turn 10 (g13: 46g against 44g, then eliminated; g12: 78g on 71g tax with the richest land), and the kit garrisons nothing, so Evil walked into one Druidic capital (g8 earlier, g11 the Undead) and the League bought two provinces from under an armyless Druid king.

**Undead: see above.** Fun to play; slow; cannot afford its own skeletons (1g each, and the Host-Leader cuts 15 to 30g of it) and cannot recover from one defeat.

**Divine: the richest realm that never gets anywhere.** Tithes gave +12 to +24g a turn by turn 12 in every game, and Holy defence plus a Proclaimed Crusade won every defence (g12: Evil's walk-in reversed at once, five Elite Pagans repelled). But most Divine realms stayed at four to nine provinces (twelve once, in g16): repelled early, then boxed in by neighbours it was at peace with, and every consecrated province behind the host was walked into by the League or Evil (g13: five times). The capital was lost to a walk-in in g10. Divine Power came, from 2 to 10, and changed nothing a player could see.

## Problems a player would hit (new since the overnight games)

1. **Nothing reports Divine Power** or names a gift when it comes. The hand-play view now prints it; the portal and the turn report should.
2. **The Skeletal Armies cast chance ignores the kit's barrow bonus** ("Necromancy 5: 2 trained, +3 from items: 50%"). The player sees the barrows raise the companies a cast and not the odds, and the text says the barrows add Necromancy.
3. **Capitals fall to walk-ins with no warning.** At least four capitals were taken by a hero stepping into an empty province (Evil's, Divine's, two Druidic). Nothing tells the owner the turn before that the capital is bare and an enemy stands next door.
4. **Two hosts entering the same province in one phase attack separately** unless a hidden "link up" roll succeeds; the Undead lost eight of thirteen that way. The roll should be shown, or the hosts should wait for each other.
5. **A 'treat' sent by both sides in the same turn makes an alliance** with no line in either report, and the alliance then turns a march back at the border. Peace needs three offers otherwise (an overture is answered only by a Treat in the next orders).
6. **A Proclaimed Crusade and a march are refused at a province a third realm took the same turn** ("turned back at the border: at peace"), with no refund of the 6g or the three turns.
7. **The Trade Pact refusal names every condition and never the one that applied.** The pact's price is shown only on refusal.
8. **Ransom:** a captive gave no orders yet "sprang from cover", attacked her captor and escaped; the ambush from a Defend order bypasses Ransom; the loser's realm cannot pay the ransom from its orders.
9. **The storm, an Encounter chase and a pursuit all work across a border at peace.** The storm's result line never names the companies hit.
10. **Hero-led skeletons show "+30%", the king's "+50%"**, where the kit text says ×1.5 under the king or a hero; "Druid shortfall −20%" is printed for the kinship grudge on ordinary companies, every battle.
11. **Recruit orders** take one company of a type per province per hero per turn, cap silently at the lead limit, and print nothing at a province of another race whose unit types the player cannot see.
12. **A king on 'accept' dies to a wandering guardian's duel** (two games, turn 3 and turn 6) and the heir needs a skill allocation the player is never asked for; lairs with no tier shown (Rock Giant, Hill Giant Chieftain) killed Melee 6 heroes.
13. **"cannot put to sea"** costs a phase for a planned path two hexes out; a move into a sea hex made a "coastal landing" instead of being refused.
14. **The Undead's unpaid-wages line says "the skeletal dead ask for nothing"** while the bill above charges 1g each; a dead necromancer's skeletal garrison vanishes without a line.
15. **Dark Pact "needs a monster province"** at a Mammoth province: nothing in the view says which Primal kinds are creatures and which are monsters.
16. **A successful Skeletal Armies roll on an empty field prints the success and raises nothing**; a king on Defend in his capital rides out to a neighbour's defence and leaves the capital empty for the phase; "2 neutral defenders" in open terrain repelled four-to-six-company hosts nine times in one game and the player cannot tell a pair of Veteran Titan Warbeasts from two levies.
17. **Shield of the Realm applied in a province Good had captured that same turn**; Proclaim Crusade fails on the turn war is declared ("none answers"); a lone monster garrison reads as an easy "1 defender".

## What I would change next (round 25)

1. **Let the barrow bonus count toward the Skeletal Armies cast chance**, or start the Undead king at Necromancy 2 trained, and place a barrow within two hexes of every Undead capital. The opening should be slow, not a coin toss. (And show the barrow bonus in the cast line.)
2. **Give the Undead a way back from one defeat:** a necromancer who ends the turn on a battlefield raises one grave free (the carters' work without the order), or the spell never fizzles at a held barrow. Keep Bind to Crack. Skeletons at 1g for two would let the realm afford to bind.
3. **Give Evil its monster province or a substitute:** the Dark Pact may name any monster kind, at double price, while the realm holds no monster province; and monster companies draw half wages while the realm holds the province that bred them. Three of fifteen Evil realms were wiped out or left with one province, each after the wage bill or one lost assault.
4. **A capital keeps a watch.** Either every capital starts with a free 1g garrison company that cannot be led away, or the report warns the turn before: "your capital has no garrison and an enemy host stands next door". At least four capitals fell to walk-ins in nine games.
5. **Slow the League's buying of other realms' land:** no pact on a province taken this turn or last, and a price per defending company that tracks the defenders' strength, not 3g flat. The unclaimed-land pact is fine.
6. **Take the Villages back a little:** +3 on tax 4 or less and +2 on tax 5, so Good's land income stops outrunning everyone else's. Keep the Beacons muster exactly as it is.
7. **Tie Knights errant to Melee only**, or stop the doubled Tactical gain at 5. A Good king at Tactical 9 by turn 12 from neutral battles is the strongest piece on the board.
8. **Count three consecrations per point of Divine Power, not two** (five of the nine games reached Power 6 or more), or cap what consecration gives at +2, now that the sword consecrates every conquest under a Crusade; and **make the gifts do something a player sees:** at Power 2 a Divine host on consecrated ground could heal a quarter between turns; at Power 4 Pilgrim Roads should apply from any consecrated province, not only the one the host started in. And report the Power.
9. **The defender's swing** decided more battles than any kit (thirteen attackers losing eight to none, three times in nine games). Every realm learned not to attack a held province without a multiplier of its own, and the wars stalled. Not a kit change, but it caps what the kits can show.
10. **Give a big treasury something to buy.** Four realms held 400 to 660g at turn 16 in g16 and three in g12, with nothing that wins a battle to spend it on. A higher recruit cap per province for gold, or kit orders with a bigger tier at a higher price, would turn hoards into play.
11. **For ten-realm games, a smaller board or more turns:** on 16 by 11 nobody meets before turn 6 and half the map is neutral at turn 16, so the result is a race against garrisons. A 14 by 9 board, or 24 turns, would test the kits against each other.
