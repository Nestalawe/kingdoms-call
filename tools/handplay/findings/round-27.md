## The short version

- **Five ten-realm games, 20 turns each. Good won four of them and the Undead won one.** It is the Undead's first win in 28 ten-realm games. Good's wins came from a king who rarely loses, steady Villages income and walking into empty land late, not from any round-27 change.
- **The Trade Pact fix works.** In every game the owner answered the notice by marching a hero in, and the seal was refused.
- **Ransom fires now, but it is the wrong size.** It fired in four of five games. The gold is trivial (8 to 13g, paid the same turn), while the hidden cost is large: the captive's surviving companies all scatter.
- **The storm caps held** in about thirty casts. The remaining complaint is that the same province can be stormed every turn.
- **The barrow guarantee changed nothing on these five seeds**, because each already had a barrow near the Undead capital. It is insurance for seeds like last round's.
- **Evil had a bad round**: no wins, and three of eight Evil realms wiped out, mostly broke on monster wages from turn 8.
- **Druidic is not a runaway in a player's hands.** Left alone it stalls on wages by turn 11. I would not change it.

## What this is

Round 27 took the six suggestions from the round-26 games and played five ten-realm games for 20 turns: all seven alignments plus three more (never a second Divine or Undead), every realm hand-played, every surviving realm giving orders every turn. Usage limits stopped the games twice; each was resumed where it stopped and played to turn 20. The bot matrix (240 games) was played once more as a second reading.

## What the games showed, change by change

**The pact sealed as the third order: fixed.** In every game where it came up, the owner read the notice, marched a hero in with his first or second order, and the seal was refused for the right reason. Two side effects showed. The notice costs the League nothing, so it can be used to pin an owner's companies in place for several turns (g34). And answering two notices at once cost one Evil realm its capital, left bare (g35).

**Ransom after a won battle: it fires now, and it is the wrong size at both ends.** It fired in four games of five. But a ransom of 8 to 13g is paid the same turn, so the captive is free at once and the gold is nothing: one Undead hero was taken three times in four turns (g34), and one Good hero twice in three turns (g37). The real penalty is hidden in the rule: what is left of the captive's column scatters. A Divine hero who lost 14 of 22 companies also lost the 8 that survived (g36), and one Pagan realm fell from 16 companies to 5 in a turn (g35). The freed hero then stands in the captor's province, with no army, in the land he has just lost. And the bar of half the companies missed three clear routs in g33, including a beaten hero with nowhere to retreat.

**The barrow near the Undead capital.** On all four seeds the ordinary lots had already put a barrow within two hexes, so the guarantee changed nothing here; it is insurance for the seeds like last round's g29. With a barrow in reach the Undead had 10 skeletons by turn 4 (g33) or 34 by turn 9 (g34). In g33 they won, their first win in 28 ten-realm games (it came in the 24th), by walking into about fifteen empty provinces while losing four of the hard battles they fought. In g34 they peaked at 16 provinces and fell to 3: the king's host was far away, the barrows stood empty, and Good took seven provinces, four without a fight. The one-company altar promotion never fired: the Undead host is skeletons, which never change quality, so the rule only matters for living companies in the column.

**The storm's caps.** They held in every one of about thirty casts: never more than two destroyed and four demoted. The caster's line now names the companies. The victim's line still gives counts only. With the caps the storm is fair per cast, and the remaining complaint is repetition: one Pagan realm stormed the same Divine host four turns running for 8g a turn (g35).

**No Dark Pact below half HP.** It was tested once and worked ("the pact wants strong blood", g36). But that king never got back above half, so the pact stayed closed for the last five turns while his realm fell from 15 provinces to 2. Evil had a bad round for another reason: three of its realms sat at 0g from turn 8 on monster and pact wages, and it won no game.

**Druidic left alone.** Not a runaway in play. It reached 13 to 17 provinces by turn 10 or 11 when nobody attacked it, and then stalled on wages (122g of wages against 82g of tax in g34; 0g for two turns). It finished second once and fifth once. The bots' 37% is not what a player gets, because bots do not pay for its over-large hosts the way a player has to.

**Good won four games of five.** Not through anything new: a king who rarely loses, the Villages' steady income, Reclaim, and walking into empty provinces late in the game. In g36 it reached 28% of the world's tax, the first realm to pass the 25% crown bar in a 20-turn game; in g35 and g37 it won narrowly on the last turns. Nothing in the game checks a leader once it is ahead.

## Problems a player would hit (new in round 27)

1. **A ransom scatters the captive's whole surviving column**, and the report does not say how many companies were lost that way.
2. **A freed captive reappears in the captor's province**, in land his realm has just lost, with no army. A captive whose realm has no gold stayed held for nine turns.
3. **The storm's line to the victim names no companies**; a storm still works from stones in a province the Pagan realm has since lost; nothing stops the same province being stormed every turn.
4. **The pact notice is free and has no limit**, so it can be used only to pin an owner's hero. Its quoted price counts tax the province does not pay yet.
5. **A Crusade can be proclaimed again the turn after it ends**, and "Crusade: holy ground ×1.5" still shows after it has ended.
6. **Blessed levies of another race fight at −20% under the Divine king**, and the recruit order does not say so.
7. **The bare-capital warning named a host of a realm at peace** (g34) and did not fire for three capitals that fell in g33, where the attacker started the turn two provinces away.
8. **A repelled king's later moves run from where he retreated to** (seen again: it walked an Undead king into an empty enemy capital).
9. **The Reckoning's refusal quotes a share that does not match the tax share the realm sees** (19% against a 25% bar, with a land-tax share of 21.8% and a log line saying the crown's price was 30%).
10. **Sea provinces count as provinces** in the standings, which inflates an island realm's count.

## What I would change next (round 28)

1. **Resize Ransom.** The captive is held for one full turn before his realm may pay. The price is 3g a skill level. He is freed at his own capital. His surviving companies fall back leaderless to the nearest province of his realm instead of vanishing. The rule also covers a beaten hero with nowhere to retreat.
2. **No storm on the same province two turns running**, and the victim's line names the companies hit.
3. **A pact notice costs the League 3g**, and a League realm may have only one notice standing at a time.
4. **Give Evil room to breathe.** A Dark Pact company draws no wages for its first three turns, and the pact is refused below a third of the king's HP, not half. Evil went from two wins to none in a round with the cooldown, the HP floor and garrison-only half wages all on.
5. **Trim Good one step.** The Villages pay +2 on tax 4 or less and +1 on tax 5. Good won four of five games and has not been wiped out in 19 games. I would leave the Beacons, the Shield and Knights errant alone.
6. **A question for you, not a change I would make alone: Defend riding out.** A host on Defend that rides into a neighbouring province to meet an attack won almost every such battle in g37, and attackers lost nearly every contested fight from turn 9. Options: (a) leave it; (b) the riding host gets no terrain bonus in the province it rides to; (c) Defend holds its own province unless the player ticks "ride to a neighbour's aid". I lean to (b). In game terms: a Good king on Defend in his forest capital who rides out to a plain next door would fight there without the forest.
7. **A second question: empty provinces.** Walk-ins into empty land still decide games (the Undead's win, Good's late land in three games). Options: (a) leave it, since the sight rule and the capital warning already help; (b) every province a realm has held for three turns keeps a free Green militia company that cannot be led away. I lean to (a) until the playtesters have answered on surprise wars, because (b) slows every war.
8. **Leave as they are:** the pact's third-order seal, the storm caps, the barrow guarantee, Druidic, the Beacons, Divine Power.
